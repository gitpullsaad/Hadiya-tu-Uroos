## Preface (پیش لفظ, p.5–6)
- Author and friends planned a campaign against un-Islamic wedding customs in Pakistan; author wrote two books then (on dowry, and on wedding customs) that weren't published at the time.
- Gap identified: no Urdu book covering marriage/family issues directly from Qur'an & Sunnah *and* suited to Pakistani society. Criticises *Tuhfat-ul-Uroos* (Urdu translation of an Arabic work) as not matching Pakistani context and for holding that ring-shaped (حلقہ دار) gold jewellery is haram for women.
- Aim: a comprehensive book from choosing a spouse to walima, rights of spouses/newborn/in-laws, divorce, iddat etc., including fatwas of Arab scholars translated into Urdu. Intended as a wedding gift and an "encyclopedia" of marital and women's issues.

# Chapter 1 — The Need for Marriage, and the Harms of Abandoning It (p.23–74)

## Section 1: Need and importance of marriage

### 1. A natural need (p.25–27)
- Islam is the religion of fitrah; marriage meets a natural need. Allowed for all, and for some adults with means, it can become **obligatory (فرض)**.
- Evidence: An-Nisa 4:3 (marry 2, 3, 4; if you fear injustice, one). Hadith: "O young men, whoever can afford it should marry — it lowers the gaze and guards chastity; whoever cannot, should fast, for it is a shield" (B 5066, M 1400 etc.).
- Analogy: sexual desire is like hunger; marriage is its proper "food". Other outlets (masturbation, zina, homosexuality) are forbidden. Al-Isra 17:32 ("do not approach zina"), An-Nur 24:2 (100 lashes for unmarried; married adulterer stoned — B 6830).
- Hadith of Abu Dharr (M 1006): intimacy with one's wife is a sadaqah/reward — if done in haram it is sin, so in halal it earns reward.

### 2. A social need (p.28–31)
- Continuation of the human race since Adam & Hawwa is through marriage; the author mentions the reported practice of Adam's children marrying across twin-pairs (not detailed in Qur'an/sahih hadith).
- Verses: An-Nisa 4:1, An-Nahl 16:72, Al-Baqarah 2:223, 2:187.
- Hadith: "Marry the loving, child-bearing woman, for I will boast of your numbers" (AD 2050, N; Ma'qil b. Yasar: a man asked about a beautiful, noble but barren woman — the Prophet ﷺ said no, three times).

### 3. A moral need (p.32–34)
- Suppressing desire is unnatural; uncontrolled release corrupts society. Islam channels it via marriage. An-Nur 24:32 ("marry off the single among you").
- "O young men…" hadith again. Three whom Allah has a right to help: the mujahid, the mukatab slave paying for freedom, and the one marrying for chastity (T 1655, N, IM 2518).
- Jabir: the Prophet ﷺ saw a woman, went to Zaynab; "a woman comes in the form of Satan… if one of you is attracted, let him go to his wife, for she has the same" (M 1403, T 1158, AD 2151).

### 4. A spiritual/psychological need (p.35)
- Spouses as a source of tranquillity: Al-A'raf 7:189, Ar-Rum 30:21 (love and mercy between spouses).

### 5. A religious need (p.36–37)
- Marriage is Sunnah: "Whoever turns away from my Sunnah is not of me" (B 5063, M 1401); "Nikah is my Sunnah…" (IM 1846 — author notes weak per Busiri).
- "When a servant marries he has completed half his religion; let him fear Allah in the other half" (Tabarani Awsat; Albani: hasan by supporting chains). Similar: whom Allah gives a righteous wife, He has helped with half his deen.

### 6. Virtue of marriage (p.38–39)
- Hadith of the poor Companions: tasbih, takbir, tahlil, enjoining good — and intimacy with one's wife — are all sadaqah (M 1006).
- Spending on family, even a morsel in the wife's mouth, is sadaqah (B 56, M 1628).
- Ibn Abbas to Sa'id b. Jubayr: "Marry, for the best of this Ummah had the most wives" (B 5069). Author: more wives isn't automatically better; Qur'an/Sunnah cap at four.

## Section 2: Ways of avoiding marriage and their consequences (p.40–63)

### 1. Sufism, monasticism, celibacy (p.40–43)
- Some think staying single gives more time for worship; this is a Christian monastic idea that led to immorality in monasteries (cites Maududi's Tafhim-ul-Qur'an). Hinduism/Buddhism similarly.
- Prophet ﷺ forbade celibacy: Abu Hurayrah asked to be castrated — "the pen has dried…" (B 5076). Ibn Mas'ud: forbidden to castrate (B 5075; Al-Ma'idah 5:87). Sa'd b. Abi Waqqas: Uthman b. Maz'un was refused permission for celibacy (B 5073, M 1402).
- The three men (one prays all night, one fasts always, one avoids women): "I fast and break fast, pray and sleep, and marry women; whoever turns from my Sunnah is not of me" (B 5063, M 1401).

### Harms of abandoning marriage (p.43–44)
1. Many medical illnesses result from not marrying.
2. Desire then pushes to illicit means → moral corruption (example of monks; Ibn al-Jawzi in *Talbis Iblis* on Sufis who abandoned marriage and fell into sin, incl. with boys).
3. Social harm: stops the continuation of the human race; contrary to Allah's design.

### 2. Avoiding marriage because zina is easy (p.45–57)
- Some avoid marriage's responsibilities (maintenance, children's upbringing, housing) and satisfy desire in brothels; they mock marriage ("why keep a buffalo when milk is sold in the market?"). Author: this mindset comes from Western "freedom" ideas now spreading in Muslim lands.
- **Roots in the West:**
  - *Freedom of thought* (حریت فکر) after the French Revolution → morality seen as a barrier to progress (quotes Pierre Louÿs' *Aphrodite*, a French novelist calling marriage barbaric, German socialist Bebel) — via Maududi's *Purdah*.
  - *Malthus (1798) and Neo-Malthusianism*: population outgrowing resources → birth control; contraceptives removed the last natural barrier (fear of pregnancy) to illicit relations.
  - *Industrialists/capitalists*: women pulled into factories/offices; clubs, cinemas, dance halls exploit desire.
  - *Misuse of scientific inventions*: radio, TV, VCR, computer spreading obscenity into homes.
  - *Co-education*: mixing of sexes in schools/universities; cites European reports of most students having sexual experience before graduation.
- **Consequences of permissiveness** (mostly from Maududi's *Purdah*; author also refers to Maulana Abdul Rahman Kilani's *Ahkam-e-Satr wa Hijab*):
  1. Spread of indecency/prostitution (London figures).
  2. Sexualised environment harming children (Dr. Edith Hooker, *Laws of Sex*); early puberty.
  3. Mass sale of contraceptives.
  4. Venereal diseases (syphilis/gonorrhoea statistics from US/UK, 1928 New York report).
  5. Forms of sexual relations: without marriage, mistresses, "trial marriage", marriage dissolved at will, homosexuality.
  6. Breakdown of family system, divorce cases, disputes over inheritance/lineage.
  7. Business of abortion.
  8. Killing of maternal instinct and of children (infanticide cases in France).
  9. Plight of elderly parents (old-age homes).
  10. End of respect for women.
- Hadith (Tirmidhi): "When your affairs are in the hands of your women, the belly of the earth is better for you than its surface."
- *The West's reconsideration*: an American magazine on the "satanic trinity" of obscene literature, films, and women's degrading dress → decline of civilisations like Rome.

### 3. Abandoning marriage for lack of means — and ways to stay chaste (p.58–63)
- Unlike the first two, this is a genuine excuse: (a) physical inability (impotence/illness) — not blameworthy; if such a man marries anyway, the wife can seek **khul'**; (b) financial inability — Shari'ah allows delay until able, but requires chastity. An-Nur 24:33: "Let those who cannot marry keep chaste until Allah enriches them."
- **Ways to stay chaste:**
  1. Fast frequently ("O young men…" hadith).
  2. Avoid anything that arouses desire: obscene literature, songs, films, dramas, etc. An-Nur 24:19.
  3. Lower the gaze from non-mahrams: An-Nur 24:30–31. "Zina of the eyes is looking" (B). To Ali: "Do not follow a glance with another; the first is forgiven, not the second" (T, AD).
  4. Keep righteous company: "A man is on the religion of his friend…" (AD 4833, T 2378); perfume-seller vs blacksmith parable (B 2101, M 2628); "Befriend only a believer, and let only the pious eat your food" (AD 4832, T 2395).
  5. Follow medical advice (consult a qualified doctor; some medicines reduce desire).
  6. Avoid idleness and solitude.
  7. Taqwa and fear of Allah — example of Prophet Yusuf refusing the wife of al-Aziz (Surah Yusuf 12:23–33).

## Section 3: Obstacles to marriage and their solutions (p.64–74)

### 1. Education as an obstacle (p.64– )
- Common belief that marriage hinders study; parents delay children's marriages "until education is complete". Two cases: (1) lack of means/employment during study; (2) means exist but fear marriage will disrupt studies.
- Author's view: if finances are genuinely lacking, delaying is acceptable; but if means exist, delaying merely out of fear for studies is wrong — marriage actually frees the mind from sexual distraction and helps concentration. Author says he knows many who married during study and did better.
- Quotes Shaykh Abdullah Nasih Ulwan (*Marriage in Islam*): as an Al-Azhar student he thought marriage would hinder study; after marrying, he found stability and focus improved; married students around him also did well.
- Hadith (Tabarani, Hakim): "Whom Allah gives a righteous wife, He has helped him with half his religion…"

### 2. Expenses/resources as an obstacle (p.67–69)
- Delay is often due to inflated "required expenses": separate house, all wedding customs, lighting, huge guest lists, baraat, lavish meals, gifts to in-laws; on the girl's side heavy dowry, jewellery, feeding the baraat. Most of these are unnecessary.
- **Model: marriage of Ali and Fatimah** — Ali was too shy to ask; the Prophet ﷺ asked if he had anything for mahr; he had only his armour (حطمی زرہ), which was given/sold as mahr. Some reports: it bought a bed, pillow, hand-mill and two water-skins for Fatimah. No baraat, no dowry procession, no customs.
- "The best/most blessed mahr is the easiest/lightest" (Ah, Hakim). Some nikahs in the Prophet's time had mahr of a pair of shoes, a handful of dates, or teaching Qur'an surahs (T 1113, AD 2111; B 2310, M 1425).
- Walima: invite a few people per means; even serving just sherbet/milk is fine.

### 3. Shyness as an obstacle (p.70)
- Parents' duty is to arrange marriage of grown children; if a son/daughter is shy, parents should gently persuade and consider the child's preference.
- If parents neglect it, the child may (without shame, it's their right) raise it — preferably via a close relative (aunt, uncle) rather than directly, keeping respect.

### 4. Age as an obstacle (p.70–72)
- In Pakistan marriage before ~20–22 (girls) / 25–30 (boys) is considered odd; Islam encourages marrying soon after puberty ("O young men…" hadith).
- Benefits of early marriage: (1) protection from sin at peak desire — "half of faith"; (2) love and tranquillity enjoyed during youth; (3) early responsibility keeps youth away from wasteful pursuits/bad habits; (4) medically, delay causes psychological/physical problems; (5) children grow up to support you before old age; late marriage means raising young children while old.

# Chapter 2 — The Islamic Way of Marriage: From Choosing a Spouse to Walima (p.73–221)
Sections: choosing spouse & rulings; priorities in choosing; seeing the girl; marriage centres; engagement; nikah; wali & love marriage; love/court marriage; gold for women; mahr; wedding night & etiquette of intimacy; walima.

## Section 1: Choosing a spouse — rulings (p.75–86)
- Islam forbids some relationships permanently, some temporarily. All non-Muslims are forbidden **except** chaste women of Ahl al-Kitab (Jews/Christians) for Muslim men.

### Marriage with non-Muslims forbidden (p.76–78)
- Al-Baqarah 2:221: do not marry polytheist women until they believe; a believing slave is better; do not marry your women to polytheist men. Reason: they invite to the Fire; spouse's influence on faith and children.
- Al Imran 3:185 (success = saved from Fire). Al-Mumtahanah 60:10: believing women emigrants are not lawful for disbelievers nor vice versa — marriages with disbelievers were annulled.
- If a spouse becomes an apostate, the marriage is void. Someone holding kufr/shirk beliefs while calling himself Muslim — marriage with him is not valid.

### Permission to marry women of the Book (p.78–79)
- Al-Ma'idah 5:5: chaste women of those given the Book are lawful, with mahr, in marriage not fornication.
- Conditions/notes: (1) only Muslim **men** may marry kitabi women — Muslim women may never marry them; (2) only chaste kitabi women; (3) proper nikah (witnesses, wali's consent, offer/acceptance, mahr); (4) she must actually be a Jew/Christian believing in Allah, revelation, prophethood, Hereafter — not an atheist/polytheist in name; (5) if it endangers faith/children (fitnah), it is better avoided.

### Women forbidden temporarily or permanently — Qur'anic texts (p.79–82)
1. An-Nisa 4:23–24 (the list of mahrams; two sisters together; married women except captives).
2. An-Nur 24:3 — fornicator marries only fornicatress/mushrikah; forbidden to believers (detail in Ch.7).
3. Al-Baqarah 2:230 — after the third talaq she is unlawful until she marries another husband (genuine marriage; *halala* is not allowed — see Ch.5).
4. Al-Baqarah 2:235 — no nikah during iddah.
5. Combining a woman with her paternal or maternal aunt is forbidden (B 5108, M 1408).
6. After **li'an**, the couple can never remarry (AD 2250).

### Permanently forbidden (p.82–85)
- **By lineage (nasab):** mother (incl. grandmothers upward), daughter (incl. granddaughters), sister (full/half), paternal aunt, maternal aunt, brother's daughter, sister's daughter (and their descendants).
- **By breastfeeding (rada'a):** "What is forbidden by lineage is forbidden by suckling" (B 2645, M 1447). The nursing woman becomes the child's mother, her husband the father; all their corresponding relatives become mahram for the suckled child only (not for the child's siblings). Conditions: within the first **two years** (Al-Baqarah 2:233), and at least **five separate full feedings** (child leaves the breast by its own will = one feeding) (M 1452).
- **By marriage (musaharah):** wife's mother/grandmothers; wife's daughter (step-daughter) if the marriage with her mother was consummated; father's wife (stepmother, incl. grandfathers' wives); son's wife (incl. grandsons' wives).
- **By li'an:** see Ch.8.
- **Li'an procedure (p.84–85):** husband accuses wife of zina without four witnesses → before a court he testifies four times "by Allah I am truthful" and a fifth time invokes Allah's curse on himself if lying; to avert the hadd she testifies four times that he is lying and a fifth time invokes Allah's wrath on herself if he is truthful. The court separates them permanently.

### Temporarily forbidden (p.85–86)
1. Two sisters together (wife's sister) — allowed only after the wife dies or is divorced (and iddah ends).
2. A woman together with her paternal/maternal aunt (or niece) — same rule.
3. A married woman — only after she is widowed/divorced and completes iddah.
4. Any woman during her iddah (Al-Baqarah 2:235).
5. Thrice-divorced wife for the same husband — only after she genuinely marries another man (not for halala) who later divorces her or dies, and her iddah ends (2:230).
6. A woman known for zina — unless she sincerely repents.

## Section 2: Choosing a spouse — priorities (p.87–97)

### Priority to religiosity (p.87–89)
- Choose a spouse for deen and taqwa, since spouses deeply influence each other and the children.
- "If someone whose religion and character you are pleased with proposes, marry [your daughter] to him; otherwise there will be fitnah and great corruption on earth" (T 1084; author notes chain discussed but meaning is supported).
- "A woman is married for four: wealth, lineage, beauty, religion — choose the religious one, may your hands be dusted" (B 5090, M 1466).
- Four things of happiness: righteous wife, spacious home, good neighbour, comfortable ride; four of misery: bad wife, bad neighbour, bad ride, cramped home (Ah, Ibn Hibban; Albani Sahihah 282).
- Women as a trial: "I have not left after me a fitnah more harmful to men than women" (B 5096); "Beware of the world and of women; the first fitnah of Bani Isra'il was through women" (M 2742).
- "The world is provision and its best provision is a righteous wife" (M 1467).

### Stories of preferring religiosity (p.90–94)
1. **Musa and the two daughters at Madyan** (Al-Qasas 28:23–27): the father (most commentators: Shu'ayb) married his daughter to Musa — a poor stranger — for his strength and trustworthiness; mahr = 8–10 years of service.
2. **Umm Sulaym and Abu Talhah**: she refused him while he was a disbeliever; her mahr was his acceptance of Islam (N 3343). Lesson against marrying non-Muslims hoping they'll convert.
3. **Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib** married his learned, beautiful daughter to his poor student Abdullah ibn Wada'ah (whose wife had just died) for a mahr of 2–3 dirhams, and brought her to his house himself that evening — having refused the Caliph Abd al-Malik's proposal for his heir al-Walid.

### Other priorities alongside religiosity (p.94–97)
1. **A virgin** — "Marry virgins: sweeter mouths, more fertile wombs, content with little" (IM 1861, Albani Sahih). Jabir married a widow; the Prophet ﷺ asked why not a virgin to play with; Jabir explained he needed someone to look after his young sisters (B 2097, M 715) — so marrying a widow/divorcee for a good reason is fine. The Prophet's ﷺ only virgin wife was Aisha.
2. **Fertile, loving woman** — "Marry the loving, child-bearing…" (AD 2050, N); Ma'qil b. Yasar's report of the barren woman.
3. **Beautiful as well as good character** — An-Nisa 4:3 ("whom you like"); "Allah is beautiful and loves beauty" (M 91). Best woman: "pleases him when he looks at her, obeys when he commands, and does not oppose him regarding herself or his wealth in ways he dislikes" (N 3233, Ah).

## Section 3: Seeing the prospective bride (p.98–102)
- Looking at a non-mahram is not allowed, but looking at a woman one intends to marry is allowed — even recommended.
- Evidence: "Go and look at her, for there is something in the eyes of the Ansar" (M 1424); Mughirah b. Shu'bah: "Look at her, it is more likely to create lasting love between you" (T 1087, N, IM 1866) — her parents hesitated but the girl herself said "if the Prophet ﷺ ordered it, look"; Jabir: "If one of you proposes and can look at what attracts him to marry her, let him do so" — Jabir hid to look at a girl (AD 2082, Ah); Muhammad b. Maslamah looked secretly — "If Allah puts the proposal in a man's heart, no harm in looking at her" (IM 1864); Ahmad: permitted even without her knowledge if intention is marriage.
- Author's view: refusing to show the girl causes problems later (divorce after marriage); better a quiet refusal before than a broken marriage after. Criticises families who let daughters go out unveiled yet object to a proposed groom seeing her once.
- **Limits:** the stronger (راجح) fiqh view: only face and hands. The girl may likewise see the boy. No private meetings or khalwah between them.

## Section 4: Marriage centres/bureaus — "killers of honour" (p.103– ) — report from monthly *Tayyibat* (Lahore, 2002)
- Daughters are a blessing, but dowry and greed make parents anxious; marriage bureaus exploit this.
- They act as businesses: exaggerated/false praise of proposals, no real knowledge of family or character; registration fees.
- Case study: a sister married through a bureau to a supposed airline employee; after the wedding he took her to his village and she disappeared/was found to be married off elsewhere — family left devastated (continues).
- More case studies (p.105–110): a bureau-arranged husband turned out to sell girls into marriages abroad; a girl whose photos taken by a bureau were later misused for blackmail, her engagement broken; a bureau ("J.K.") said to sell girls' photos abroad; internet marriage sites that publish photos; a young man married via a website to a woman who turned out much older, previously divorced with children — ended in divorce.
- Author's conclusion: bureaus exploit people for commission; trust Allah, make du'a, and use the Prophet's ﷺ criteria (deen first). The real solution is to drop artificial standards (caste, wealth, customs) that keep good girls unmarried.

## Section 5: Engagement (منگنی) and related issues (p.112–118)
- **Is engagement allowed?** Yes — a pre-agreement before nikah, supported by the proposal hadiths (Mughirah, Jabir, Muhammad b. Maslamah). But it is merely permissible, not a required part of marriage; in the Prophet's ﷺ time people went straight to nikah (sometimes with delayed rukhsati). Engagement is useful when families need time to get to know each other.
- **Engagement customs:** baraat-like processions to the girl's house, sweets, gift exchanges — wasteful customs; details in Ch.3.
- **Engagement ring:** men may wear only silver; women gold or silver. Gifting a ring is fine as a gift, but treating the ring as a *symbol/ritual of engagement* is a custom (of Christian origin) that should be dropped (Albani, *Adab al-Zifaf* p.140–142).
- **Proposing over another's proposal** is forbidden: "No man should propose over his brother's proposal until he withdraws or permits" (B 5142, M 1412); "A believer is the brother of a believer — he may not buy over his brother's sale nor propose over his proposal until he leaves it" (M 1414). Some fuqaha allow it if the first proposal is not yet agreed — Fatimah bint Qays received proposals from Mu'awiyah, Abu Jahm and Usamah at once; the Prophet ﷺ advised Usamah (M 1480). If the first proposer is irreligious/sinful, a righteous person may propose (Fath al-Bari etc.).
- **Meetings of engaged couple:** seeing once / talking in presence of others is OK, but private meetings, outings and khalwah are not — they remain non-mahram. "No man should be alone with a woman except with a mahram" (B 5233, M 1341). Phone/letter conversations also better avoided.
- **Breaking an engagement:** it is a promise; fulfilling it is a moral duty, but it may be broken for a valid reason (e.g. a defect discovered). Better to break it than to marry and divorce. Breaking an engagement is **not** a talaq (unlike ending a nikah, which requires talaq even before rukhsati).

## Section 6: Wali (guardian) as a condition of nikah, and love marriage (p.119–137)
### Who is the wali? (p.119)
- Wali = guardian. Nearest male relative: father; then paternal grandfather; then brother; then paternal uncle, etc. In some cases a grown son can be the wali of his widowed/divorced mother. If no wali exists, the ruler/state is the wali: "The sultan is the wali of one who has no wali" (AD 2083, T 1102, IM 1879).
- Wali's consent is required for every woman — adult or minor, virgin or previously married.

### Qur'anic evidence (p.120–124)
1. Al-Baqarah 2:221 — Allah addresses *men* ("do not marry [your women] to polytheists") → women's marriage is handled through guardians. Qurtubi: "a text that there is no nikah without a wali"; Ibn Taymiyyah: Allah addressed men about marrying off women; Ibn al-Arabi (*Ahkam al-Qur'an*).
2. Al-Baqarah 2:232 — "do not prevent them from marrying their husbands": revealed about **Ma'qil b. Yasar**, who refused to remarry his sister to her ex-husband; he then complied (B 5130). Ibn Kathir: proves a woman cannot marry herself off — the wali's role is required. Same view: Tabari, Qurtubi, Shafi'i, Baghawi, Ibn Hajar, Shawkani, San'ani.
3. An-Nur 24:32 — "Marry off the single among you" — addressed to guardians (Baghawi).

### Hadith evidence (p.124– )
1. Aisha on pre-Islamic marriage types: of these, the one that remained is a man proposing to the girl's wali, giving mahr and marrying her; Islam abolished the rest (B 5127).
2. "No nikah without a wali" (Abu Musa; AD 2085, T 1101, IM 1881 — sahih per Tirmidhi, Ibn Hibban, Hakim, Albani). Hakim lists ~30 Companions narrating this, including wives of the Prophet ﷺ.
3. Aisha: "Any woman who marries without her wali's permission, her nikah is void, void, void; if he consummates, she gets the mahr for what he made lawful; if they dispute, the sultan is wali of the one without a wali" (AD 2083, T 1102, IM 1879).

### Fuqaha (p.126–128)
- **Malik** (via Ibn Rushd, *Bidayat al-Mujtahid*): wali is a condition of validity. **Shafi'i**: contract without wali is void. **Ahmad** (Ibn Qudamah, *al-Mughni*): a woman cannot marry herself or another off, nor appoint anyone but her wali. **Hasan al-Basri**: no nikah without wali; if a woman marries herself off they are separated, consummated or not. **Ibrahim al-Nakha'i** (teacher-line of Abu Hanifah): "contracting is not in women's hands but men's."

### Summary & Hanafi fiqh (p.128)
- Author's conclusion: from Qur'an, Hadith and majority of scholars, the stronger view is that a woman's nikah — virgin or previously married — is **invalid without the wali's permission**.
- Hanafi view: an adult woman's nikah without wali is valid, *but* if the husband is not her **kuf'** (equal in family standing), it can be annulled. Abu Hanifah reportedly: if she married a non-kuf' without the wali, it's void. Author: the clear evidence favours the majority.

### Joint statement of contemporary scholars (p.129–132) — from *Muhaddith* (Lahore, 1996), via Dar al-Ifta (Maulana Zahid al-Rashidi)
- Title: "No sane adult Muslim girl may contract her own nikah without the consent of her family/wali."
- Summarises madhahib (from Anwar Shah Kashmiri's *Fayd al-Bari*): Malik, Shafi'i, Ahmad — adult virgin cannot marry without wali's consent; Abu Yusuf & Muhammad (Hanafi) — not without wali's consent, though she may do the offer/acceptance with his permission; Abu Hanifah — may contract herself but must observe kafa'ah, otherwise wali can go to court to annul; Ja'fari fiqh — father/grandfather's permission is a precautionary obligation for a virgin.
- Kafa'ah = what the wali/family would consider dishonourable (varies with custom).
- The statement favours a balance requiring both the girl's and family's consent; criticises Western individualism; quotes Hillary Clinton's 1995 Islamabad college visit (said Pakistani girls should keep their religious traditions). Urges courts to consider Islamic law and family system.
- Signatories across schools: Maulana Abdul Rahman Ashrafi, Dr Sarfraz Naeemi, Shia scholar Sajid Naqvi, Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Qadri, Hafiz Abd al-Rahman Madani, Abdul Malik, Hafiz Abdul Salam Fatehpuri and others (Deobandi, Barelvi, Ahl-e-Hadith, Shia, Jamaat-e-Islami).

### Why a wali for girls (p.133–134)
1. Division of roles: men handle outside affairs; men can investigate a suitor better.
2. Natural modesty makes it hard for a woman to propose for herself or bear rejection; the wali protects her.
3. Women's emotional nature; a decision this weighty benefits from the guardians' experience.

### The girl's consent is also necessary (p.134–137)
- The wali cannot force her; her consent is a condition just like the wali's — virgin or previously married. A forced marriage may be annulled by court.
- "An orphan girl is to be consulted; her silence is consent; if she refuses, she is not to be compelled" (AD 2093, T 1109).
- "A previously married woman is not married until consulted (*tusta'mar*), nor a virgin until her permission is sought (*tusta'dhan*) — her permission is her silence" (B 5136, M 1419).
- Khansa bint Khidham: married by her father against her will — the Prophet ﷺ annulled it (B 6945).
- Ibn Abbas: a virgin married off by her father against her will was given the choice by the Prophet ﷺ (AD 2096, IM 1875).
- Aisha: a girl married to her cousin to raise his status; the Prophet ﷺ gave her the choice; she said "I accept my father's act, but I wanted women to know fathers have no [unilateral] say" (N 3271, IM 1874).

## Love marriage and court marriage (p.138–144+)
### Permissible form (p.138–139)
- "Love marriage" in the sense of the boy and girl both being happy with the match, done openly via the families with wali, witnesses and mahr — this is not only allowed but liked by Islam (An-Nisa 4:3 "whom you like"). The wali should respect the girl's wish when the match is suitable; if he refuses unreasonably, the Ma'qil b. Yasar case (2:232) shows he must not obstruct.

### Impermissible form (p.140–141)
- Secret affair → elopement → court marriage without the wali: Islam considers it **void** (no nikah without wali). Islam also forbids the steps leading there (khalwah, meetings, phone affairs). Parents share blame: no Islamic upbringing, TV/VCR/films, co-education.

### Fate of girls who run away (p.142– ) — quote from Maulana Naeem Siddiqui
- Likely outcomes: abandoned after the "love" fades; family disgraced, sisters' marriages harmed; lover delays nikah and dumps her when pregnant — leading to trafficking or suicide; lover turns out already married/poor; or she is used by the man to entertain business contacts (story of a "lover" who pimps his wife to a foreign guest).
- Further outcomes: some end in brothels or are sold; many commit suicide; a few return to their parents; a small number get the parents to accept the match after a child is born. Siddiqui estimates no more than ~5% end successfully (source: Naeem Siddiqui, *Ghar se Mafroor Larkiyon ke Ishq-bazana Nikah*, p.20–23).

## Section 7: The nikah and related issues (p.147–163)
- **Wali's permission:** required for the bride (not for the groom). His permission is enough — he need not be physically present; he can be represented, or give written consent.
- **Bride's consent:** required; if forced, she has the right to have it annulled by a court.
- **Witnesses (p.148–149):** at least two upright Muslim witnesses. "Prostitutes are those who marry without proof (witnesses)" (T 1103); "No nikah without a wali and two upright witnesses" (Ibn Hibban, Daraqutni); Umar rejected a nikah witnessed by one man and one woman: "this is a secret marriage — I'd have stoned for it" (Muwatta). Tirmidhi: Companions/Successors agreed — no nikah without witnesses; differences only on whether witnesses must be present together. Some (Malik's people) accepted *public announcement* as sufficient; Ahmad/Ishaq accept one man + two women.
- **Offer & acceptance (ijab/qabul) (p.149–151):** consent of both is required. Groom accepts verbally in front of witnesses ("I accept"); the wali or his representative makes the offer. Signing the nikah form also indicates consent, but saying "qabul" aloud is best. Hadith of the woman who offered herself — the Prophet ﷺ married her to a man for the Qur'an he knew (B 5149, M 1425): no explicit verbal acceptance recorded, showing any clear expression of consent suffices. Abd al-Rahman b. Awf married Umm Hakim (who gave him authority) to himself (B, mu'allaq). A mute person may consent by gesture.
- **Khutbah al-Hajah (p.151–152):** the sermon of need — praise of Allah, seeking help/forgiveness, refuge from evil of selves/deeds, shahadah — followed by Al Imran 3:102, An-Nisa 4:1, Al-Ahzab 33:70–71 (AD 2118, T 1105, N, IM 1892; authenticated by Albani as a whole, who wrote a treatise on it).
- **How to conduct the nikah (p.153):** after the khutbah, the officiant says (in substance): "I marry so-and-so daughter of so-and-so, by her wali's permission and her consent, to so-and-so son of so-and-so, before these witnesses, for this mahr" — then asks the groom "Do you accept?" — he says "I accept". The bride doesn't need to say ijab/qabul herself at the ceremony; her wali/representative acts for her. Write names and mahr on paper beforehand to avoid mistakes. Giving advice to the couple/attendees is recommended. **Reciting the kalimahs to the bride and groom at the nikah is a bid'ah** (no basis).
- **Du'a for the couple:** "Barakallahu laka wa baraka 'alayka wa jama'a baynakuma fi khayr" (AD 2130, T 1091, IM 1905).
- **Nikah by telephone (p.154):** valid if the wali/representative and the groom/his agent do ijab/qabul with two upright witnesses hearing. Precaution: better for both parties and witnesses to be in one sitting; but if hard (e.g. abroad), it's allowed with care to avoid fraud. Talaq over phone likewise takes effect (see Ch.6).
- **A woman cannot conduct a nikah (p.154–155):** "A woman may not marry off a woman, nor herself" (IM 1882, Daraqutni). Aisha arranged a nikah for her nephew but had a man perform the contract: "Nikah is not for women" (Fath al-Bari, Abd al-Razzaq). Ali: "A woman doesn't give the khutbah or conduct the nikah."
- **Place: mosque or hotel? (p.155–157):** no fixed place in Shari'ah. Prophet ﷺ married Khadijah in Makkah at home; Safiyyah in the open on the way back from Khaybar. The hadith "Announce the nikah, do it in mosques, beat the daff" (T 1089) is **weak** (Isa b. Maymun — weak per Tirmidhi, Bukhari, Ibn Ma'in, Dhahabi, Ibn Hajar etc.). Mosque is fine (blessed place, saves costs of halls/hotels), but not required. Hotel allowed if Islamic limits are kept.
- **Daff and songs (p.157–159):** permitted for young girls to sing decent songs (no shirk, obscenity, arousal) and beat the **daff** (one-sided frame drum without jingles). Dhol (two-sided) and other instruments are not allowed; daff for men not allowed. Evidence: "The difference between halal and haram is the daff and voice" (T 1088, N, IM 1896); Rubayyi' bint Mu'awwidh — girls beat daff singing of Badr martyrs; the Prophet ﷺ stopped the line "among us is a Prophet who knows tomorrow" (B 5147); Aisha — "Had you no entertainment? The Ansar like it" (B 5162); suggested verse "Atainakum atainakum…". Using these to justify music bands is wrong (Ch.3).
- **Gifts (p.159–160):** "Give gifts to one another, you will love one another" (Bukhari *Adab al-Mufrad* 594; hasan per Ibn Hajar). Bukhari's chapter "Gift for the bride": Umm Sulaym sent hays (dates/ghee/cheese dish) via Anas at the Prophet's ﷺ marriage to Zaynab; it miraculously fed many (B 5163). Khadijah gave Zaynab a necklace at her wedding.
- **Food at the nikah (p.161):** the girl's family may feed guests (hospitality — "whoever believes… honour his guest", B 6019, M) — not bid'ah, not required. Condemns huge baraat descending on the bride's family; middle way: few close people, simple food per means.
- **Adornment of bride and groom (p.162–163):** Islam encourages beauty within limits ("Allah is beautiful…", M 91). Borrowing clothes/jewellery for a wedding is allowed (Aisha borrowed Asma's necklace, B 5164). Men's perfume: scent noticeable, colour not; women's: colour, faint scent (T 2787, N 5117). Aisha adorned an Ansari bride (B 5162). Gold and silk lawful for women, forbidden for men (T 1720, N 5151). Best dye for white hair: henna and katam (AD 4205, T 1753, IM 3622).

## Section 8: Gold for the bride and women (p.164–173)
- Women using gold jewellery has been accepted throughout Islamic history; a recent view (Shaykh **Nasir al-Din al-Albani**) held that **ring-shaped (encircling) gold** — rings, bangles, necklaces — is haram for women, only non-encircling gold allowed. The author respects Albani but sees this as one of his isolated views (tafarrudat). Presents **Shaykh Ibn Baz's** fatwa refuting it (translated; from *Fatawa al-Mar'ah al-Muslimah*).
- **Question to Ibn Baz:** Albani in *Adab al-Zifaf* declared encircling gold haram, causing doubt among women; is it permitted?
- **Ibn Baz's answer (p.165–173):** all gold — encircling or not — is lawful for women.
  - Az-Zukhruf 43:18 ("one brought up in ornaments") — jewellery described as a trait of women, unrestricted.
  - Ali: the Prophet ﷺ took silk in his right hand and gold in his left — "These two are haram for the males of my Ummah" (AD, N; IM adds "lawful for its females"). Abu Musa: "Gold and silk are lawful for the females of my Ummah, haram for its males" (Ah, N, T, AD, Hakim). The claimed break in Abu Musa's chain is answered by supporting narrations.
  - Scholars: **al-Jassas** (*Ahkam al-Qur'an*) — reports allowing it are stronger and more famous; **Kiya al-Harrasi** — ijma' on women's jewellery; **Bayhaqi** — prohibition reports are abrogated by ijma'; **Nawawi** (*al-Majmu'*, *Sharh Muslim*) — ijma' that women may wear gold of all kinds: rings, necklaces, bangles, anklets, etc.; **Ibn Hajar** (*Fath al-Bari*) — ijma' that the prohibition of the gold ring is only for men.
  - Further hadith: (1) a woman with her daughter wearing two thick gold bangles — the Prophet ﷺ asked whether she paid zakat on them, warning of bracelets of fire; he did not object to wearing them (AD, N via Amr b. Shu'ayb); (2) the Negus sent jewellery including a gold ring; the Prophet ﷺ gave it to his granddaughter Umamah: "adorn yourself with this" (AD, sound chain) — an encircling gold ring; (3) Umm Salamah wore gold ornaments (awdah) and asked if it was *kanz*; "If you pay its zakat it is not kanz" (AD, Daraqutni; Hakim sahih).
  - Reports that seem to prohibit are **shadh** (contradicting stronger reports) or abrogated; Albani's reconciliation (prohibition = encircling, permission = non-encircling) fails because the permitting reports themselves include rings and bangles.
  - Conclusion: gold of every kind is lawful for women, haram for men.

## Section 9: Mahr (dower) and related issues (p.174–185)
- Islam gave women the right of mahr — money/property the husband must give at marriage per his means. Only the wife can waive it (fully/partly) willingly; it cannot be withheld.
- **Compensation or gift? (p.174–175):** some fuqaha see it as compensation for conjugal enjoyment (An-Nisa 4:24 "give them their ujur"); others as a free gift (4:4 "nihlah"). Author prefers the **gift/honour** view — e.g. mahr (or half) is due even without consummation, and both spouses enjoy intimacy equally.
- **Fixing the mahr (p.176–178):** Al-Baqarah 2:236–237: 
  1. Nikah is valid without specifying mahr.
  2. If divorced before consummation with no mahr fixed — no mahr is due, but a gift (mut'ah) per means is required.
  3. If divorced before consummation with mahr fixed — **half** the mahr is due (unless she or the wali waives; man waiving and giving all is closer to taqwa).
  4. Mahr fixed and divorce after valid seclusion (khalwah) — full mahr.
  5. Divorce after intimacy with no fixed mahr — **mahr al-mithl** (customary mahr of women of her family).
- **Widow's mahr:** in all cases a widow gets full mahr (fixed amount, else mahr al-mithl), consummated or not. Ibn Mas'ud's ruling — mahr al-mithl, iddah and inheritance — confirmed by Ma'qil b. Sinan citing the Prophet's ﷺ judgment on Birwa' bint Washiq (T 1145, AD 2115, N, IM 1891).
- **When to pay (p.178–179):** flexible — at nikah, before/after rukhsati, or later; if he dies unpaid, it is paid from his estate. Uqbah b. Amir: a man married without mahr fixed; at death he gave his Khaybar share as her mahr; she sold it for 100,000 (AD 2117, Hakim sahih). Best: at the time of nikah or soon after.
- **No fixed amount (p.179):** Shari'ah sets no amount; depends on husband's means — large if rich; if poor, a few rupees, a ring, or teaching some Qur'an.
- **Large mahr is permitted (p.179–181):** An-Nisa 4:20 ("a qintar/heap of treasure"); Ibn Kathir: shows permissibility of a large mahr. Story: Umar told people not to exceed 400 dirhams; a Quraysh woman cited 4:20; Umar said "a woman was right and Umar wrong" and withdrew it (Ibn Kathir: chain good/strong; author notes some weakness in a narrator but the point stands). Aisha: the Prophet's ﷺ wives' mahr was 12½ uqiyah = **500 dirhams** (M 1426). Conversion given: 1 dirham ≈ 2.975 g silver; 500 dirhams ≈ 25 dinars (1 dinar ≈ 4.25 g gold); in 2003 money ≈ Rs 64,000 by gold, ≈ Rs 20–25,000 by silver.
- **Minimum (p.181–182):** no minimum: man who had only half his lower garment — "look for even an iron ring" — married for the Qur'an he knew (B 5030, M 1425). "Mahr not less than 10 dirhams" is weak.
- **Less is better (p.183):** "The most blessed nikah is the one with least burden" (Ah — weak chain, supported); "Best nikah is the easiest" (AD 2117, Ibn Hibban, Hakim); "Best mahr is the easiest".
- **Myth of "Rs 32 Shar'i mahr" (p.183–185):** In Pakistan lakhs are spent on customs but mahr is set at "Shar'i mahr = Rs 32 and 8 annas". Origin: Hanafi minimum of 10 dirhams (based on a weak hadith), converted to rupees long ago. Problems: (1) the 10-dirham hadith is weak; (2) value of 10 dirhams today is far higher (~Rs 1,000 per some scholars; quotes Abdul Rahman Kilani's *Taysir al-Qur'an*); (3) 10 dirhams was a *minimum*, not a standard; (4) if following Sunnah, the Prophet's ﷺ mahr (500 dirhams) would be ~Rs 60,000 — which isn't obligatory either; it should match the husband's means and mutual agreement.
- **Wrong practices (p.185):** fixing an unaffordable mahr with no intention to pay ("who pays it anyway?"), or pressuring the wife to waive it; setting a huge mahr as a weapon against divorce; families keeping the mahr themselves; using high mahr demands that keep daughters unmarried. Correct: mahr belongs to the woman; wali cannot take it; she may waive part/all only by her own free will — An-Nisa 4:4 ("if they remit any of it willingly, enjoy it").
- Summary: no fixed max/min; set per means; a lower mahr is preferable.

## Section 10: Wedding night and etiquette of intimacy (p.186–208)
- **Comforting the bride (p.186–187):** she is anxious entering a new home; the groom/household should welcome her, offer food, ease her nervousness. Asma bint Yazid: at Aisha's wedding the Prophet ﷺ brought milk, drank, offered it to Aisha (shy), then to the women; "do not combine hunger and lying" (when they declined while wanting it) (Ah; Albani: hasan with support).
- **Du'a on first meeting (p.187–188):** place hand on her forehead, say Bismillah and ask Allah for her good and the good she was created with, and seek refuge from her evil (AD 2160, IM 1918, Hakim). Explain to the bride beforehand so she isn't hurt by the wording.
- **Prayer together (p.188–189):** recommended to pray two rak'ahs together on the first night. Ibn Mas'ud advised Abu Hariz, fearing his young bride might dislike him: love is from Allah, aversion from Shaytan; pray two rak'ahs with her behind you and make du'a for blessing in each other (Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Abi Shaybah, Tabarani; in *Adab al-Zifaf*).
- **Du'a before intimacy:** "Bismillah, Allahumma jannibna al-shaytan wa jannib al-shaytan ma razaqtana" — if a child is decreed, Shaytan won't harm it (B 141, M 1434).
- **Considerate approach (p.189–190):** a woman needs more emotional preparation; the husband should not rush to fulfil his own need but ensure her satisfaction too — affection, play, kissing, gradually.
- **Advice from a doctor (p.190–193)** (Dr. Farid, *Hamari Jinsi Zindagi*): ignorance of sex education on wedding night can cause trauma, fights, even psychiatric problems; couples shouldn't follow crude advice from friends; no shame/pressure to complete consummation on the first night. Practical points: go gently over days; rest after bleeding; abstain until healed; hygiene supplies (husband's responsibility); use a lubricant if needed; if impotence persists, see a doctor; nutritious diet; avoid worry/anxiety; avoid habits causing weakness.
- **Lawful manner (p.193–195):** any position is allowed but intercourse only in the vagina; **anal intercourse is haram**. Al-Baqarah 2:223 ("your wives are a tilth… approach as you wish") refers to positions, not the anus. Jews claimed a child is cross-eyed if intercourse is from behind — the verse rebutted it (B 4528, M 1435). "Cursed is he who goes to a woman in her rear" (AD 2162, IM 1923, Ah). Ibn Abbas: the Ansar (influenced by Jews) used one position; a Muhajir married an Ansari woman who objected → verse revealed (AD 2164).
- **Nakedness between spouses (p.195–196):** allowed; no authentic prohibition on seeing each other's awrah. Bahz b. Hakim: "Guard your awrah except from your wife or slave-woman… Allah is more deserving of modesty" (AD 4017, T 2769, IM 1920). Covering is recommended when alone; cover from others/children.
- **Refusing without excuse (p.196–197):** wife shouldn't refuse without a valid reason. "Whoever calls his wife to bed and she refuses, the One above the heavens is displeased with her until he is pleased" (M 1436); "the angels curse her till morning" (B 5193, M 1436).
- **Reward:** intimacy with one's wife is sadaqah (M 1006, Abu Dharr).
- **Timing (p.198):** no fixed time (except during menses/postnatal bleeding); best when desire arises. "A woman should not fast (voluntarily) while her husband is present except with his permission" (B 5195, M 1026).
- **Friday night/day (p.198–199):** hadith of Aws b. Aws "whoever *ghassala* and bathes on Friday…" — understood by some as intimacy before Jumu'ah — reward of a year's fasting and prayer per step (T 496, AD 345, N, IM 1087); Abu Hurayrah: "whoever does ghusl janabah on Friday then goes early is like one who sacrificed a camel…" (B 881, M 850).
- **Revealing bedroom secrets is haram (p.199–200):** "Among the worst people before Allah on the Day of Resurrection is a man who is intimate with his wife and she with him, then he spreads her secret" (M 1437). Author warns against husbands describing wives' beauty to friends, and women gossiping about wedding nights.
- **Wudu/ghusl before repeating (p.200–201):** "If one of you goes to his wife then wants to return, let him do wudu" (M 308; Hakim: it makes one more energetic). Abu Rafi': the Prophet ﷺ visited his wives and bathed after each — "purer and better" (AD 219, IM 590).
- **Bathing together (p.201–202):** Aisha and the Prophet ﷺ bathed from one vessel (B 263, M 321). Touching a woman doesn't break wudu. The report "I never saw the Prophet's ﷺ private parts" is fabricated (Barakah b. Muhammad al-Halabi, a liar) — Albani.
- **Sunnah ghusl janabah (p.202–203)** (Aisha, M 316, B 248): wash hands; wash private parts with left hand; full wudu; run wet fingers through hair roots; pour three handfuls on the head; wash whole body; (feet at end or with wudu). A woman need not undo braids for ghusl janabah, but should for ghusl after menses/nifas.
- **Wudu before sleeping while junub (p.203):** recommended (B 288, M 305; Umar asked — yes, after wudu, M 306).
- **Intercourse during menses/nifas is haram (p.204– ):** Al-Baqarah 2:222. "Whoever has intercourse with a menstruating woman, or a woman in the rear, or goes to a fortune-teller and believes him, has disbelieved in what was revealed to Muhammad ﷺ" (T 135, AD 3904, IM 639). Anas: Jews isolated menstruating women entirely; the Prophet ﷺ said "do everything except intercourse" (M 302).
- Aisha: the Prophet ﷺ would have her wear a waist-wrap during menses and be intimate except intercourse (B 302, M 293). If the wedding date falls during the bride's period, everything except intercourse is allowed. **Nifas:** intercourse forbidden until bleeding stops; max 40 days — if bleeding stops earlier, intercourse is allowed (with medical caution).
- **Expiation for intercourse during menses/nifas (p.206):** give **one dinar or half a dinar** in charity (AD 2168, T 136, N, IM 640); Ibn Abbas: one dinar if the blood is red (early), half if yellowish (late) (T 137). Medical harms of intercourse during menses noted.
- **Coitus interruptus ('azl) — allowed, but... (p.206–208):** includes modern barrier methods (condom, cervical cap). Permissible without necessity but disliked; with genuine (e.g. medical) need, no dislike. Jabir: "We practised 'azl while the Qur'an was being revealed" and the Prophet ﷺ didn't forbid it (M 1440, B 5208); but he called it "the hidden burial" (M 1442), and said any soul destined to exist will exist (B 5210).
- **Intercourse during pregnancy or breastfeeding (p.208):** allowed (only menses/nifas forbidden). The Prophet ﷺ intended to forbid *ghilah* (intercourse while nursing) but saw Romans and Persians do it without harm to children (M 1442).

## Section 11: Walima and related rulings (p.209–223)
- **What is walima:** the meal given by the groom after the marriage (from *walm* = coming together). Applies to the groom's feast; the bride's family's meal isn't called walima.
- **Ruling (p.209–210):** disputed — Zahiris, Malik (per Qurtubi), one view of Shafi'i: obligatory; Ibn Qudamah: Hanbali/most scholars — recommended. **Author's view: obligatory**, because: (1) the Prophet ﷺ commanded Abd al-Rahman b. Awf "give a walima, even with one sheep" (B 5153, M 1427); (2) the Prophet ﷺ gave walima at all his marriages; (3) no marriage in his time without walima; (4) it is not burdensome — even a glass of sherbet suffices; (5) it publicly announces the marriage.
- **Timing (p.211):** scholars differ (at nikah, after consummation, anytime). Closest to Sunnah: **after consummation** — e.g. morning after the Prophet's ﷺ marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh (B 5166, M 1428). Delaying a day or two for need is fine.
- **How many days (p.211–212):** one day is required; more days allowed if one can afford, without showing off — Bukhari's chapter: the Prophet ﷺ did not fix one or two days (some reports: up to 7).
- **What to serve (p.212–213):** meat best if affordable ("even one sheep"; Zaynab's walima had meat — B 5168, M 1428); no debt for lavish food — anything is fine. Safiyyah's walima: dates and sawiq (AD 3744, T 1095, IM 1909); another: two mudd of barley (B 5172).
- **Helping a poor groom (p.213):** the wealthy should help. On return from Khaybar (Safiyyah), the Prophet ﷺ asked everyone to bring what they had; a leather mat was spread with dates, ghee, sawiq/cheese (*hays*) — that was the walima (B 371, M 1365).
- **Whom to invite (p.214):** rich and poor; preferably the righteous ("eat only with the pious", AD 4832). **Inviting only the rich is condemned:** "The worst food is the walima food to which the rich are invited and the poor left out" (B 5177, M 1432); "...those who would come are refused and those who refuse are invited" (M 1432).
- **Accepting the invitation is obligatory, even when fasting (p.215):** "When invited to a walima, accept" (M 1429); if fasting, make du'a; if not, eat. Breaking a voluntary fast is fine (no make-up required); or one may stay fasting and attend. "Whoever refuses an invitation has disobeyed Allah and His Messenger" (B 5177, M 1432).
- **When not to attend (p.216–218):** (1) where there are munkarat — music, dhol, bands, dancing, filming — attend only if you can correct it; else attendance is haram (An-Nisa 4:140; Al-Ma'idah 5:2). (2) Where alcohol is served or the host's income is haram: "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should not sit at a table where wine is passed around" (T 2801). (3) Where pictures of living beings are displayed: Aisha's cushion with pictures — the Prophet ﷺ refused to enter; picture-makers punished; angels don't enter houses with pictures (B 5181); Ibn Mas'ud and Abu Ayyub turned back from houses with images. (4) Feasts held out of pride/showing off: the Prophet ﷺ forbade eating from those who compete in boasting (AD 3754, Albani *Sahihah* 627). (5) Valid excuse — rain, storm, illness, no transport.
- **Etiquette for guests (p.218–219):** bring a gift if possible; make du'a for the couple ("Barakallahu laka…", AD 2130); du'a for the host: "Allahumma barik lahum fima razaqtahum waghfir lahum warhamhum" (M 2042); "Allahumma at'im man at'amani wasqi man saqani" (M 2055).
- **Eating/drinking standing at weddings (p.219–223):** prohibitions — the Prophet ﷺ forbade drinking standing (M 2025); Qatadah via Anas: eating standing is "worse" (M 2024); "let whoever drinks standing vomit it" (M 2026). Permissions — Ali drank standing in Kufa saying he saw the Prophet ﷺ do so (B 5615); the Prophet ﷺ drank Zamzam standing (B 5617, M 2027); Ibn Umar: in the Prophet's ﷺ time we ate while walking and drank standing (T 1880, IM 3301). Reconciliation (Ibn Hajar via Maziri; Nawawi): majority view — permissible; prohibition = disliked (makruh tanzihi), not haram; Umar, Uthman, Ali, Sa'd, Aisha saw no harm. **Author:** sitting is preferable; standing allowed if seating isn't possible.

# Chapter 3 — Wedding Customs and Islam (p.227–282)
List of customs covered (p.225): mayun, mehndi, sehra, gani/garlands, salami, baraat & band, decorated horse/car, fireworks, muh-dikhai, god-bharai, dowry, vari & badd, neota (nyondra), photos/films, dancers, pranks on groom, visiting shrines, reciting kalimahs at nikah, shaving beard, plucking eyebrows/long nails, gold ring for groom, "marriage to the Qur'an", mixed gatherings.

## Section 1: Wedding customs (p.227–241)
- Islam makes marriage simple; we made it costly and hard with customs, delaying marriages and fueling immorality. Children's weddings are "planned" from birth.
- **Engagement customs:** large parties, sweets/gifts to all relatives, ring-exchange by the couple themselves (they're non-mahram), videos — all excess.
- **Mayun:** bride secluded in a corner for days before the wedding — a Hindu custom; no basis.
- **Tel/Mehndi:** processions carrying henna to each house with music/dance — un-Islamic. Henna for the bride is fine (recommended); for the groom (man) not appropriate as adornment.
- **Sehra (groom's head-garland):** said to originate with Iranian fire-worshippers (face covered like the sun); hint of shirk.
- **Garlands of currency notes** on the groom: wasteful, risky; even flower garlands as a ritual are a bad custom.
- **Salami** (money given to groom/bride when greeting) — a form of neota; petty.
- **Baraat:** large crowds descending on the bride's home — a Hindu custom contrary to Islam's simplicity; a few people accompanying the groom is fine.
- **Band/music** with baraat: sinful — *lahw al-hadith* (Luqman 31:6; Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas: it means singing/music).
- **Decorated horse/car for groom:** imitating non-Muslims and showing off.
- **Fireworks:** burning money; dangerous; forbidden extravagance.
- **Hijras/dancers/singers:** sin and indecency, punishable.
- **Throwing money (sadqa/vel) over the groom:** superstition and omens — not allowed.
- **Making bride/groom recite kalimahs at nikah:** no basis; implies they weren't Muslim; **bid'ah**.
- **Badd** (sweets/dry fruits distributed by groom's party) and **vari** (bridal clothes/jewellery displayed to all) — show-off customs.
- **Neota/Nyondra (p.234):** registering cash gifts at walima to be repaid (often doubled) later — mean, causes disputes; Al-Muddaththir 74:6 ("do not give to gain more"). A voluntary gift is fine if not recorded/expected back.
- **Pranks on groom** (salty milk, stealing shoes for ransom) by bride's sisters/non-mahram girls — immoral. **Sarbala** (a boy accompanying groom) — Hindu-origin custom, no basis.
- **Jahez demand** — Hindu custom (details Section 3).
- **Sending bride off under the Qur'an** held over her head — superstition; blessing is in acting on it.
- **Oil on doorframe**, **kangna (wrist thread)** — ignorant customs.
- **Visiting shrines (mazars)** with the couple for blessings — shirk; good/harm only from Allah.
- **Muh-dikhai** (unveiling bride's face for gifts), including non-mahram men — wrong. **God-bharai** (putting a baby boy in bride's lap for fertility) — superstition; sometimes even by non-mahram brother-in-law. **Chauthi** (bride returning to parents on 4th day) — needless custom; depends on husband's permission.
- **"Marriage to the Qur'an" — Sindh (p.238–241)** (report from *Takbeer*, 1995): some waderas/pirs, to keep land and status within the family, "marry" daughters to the Qur'an (*haq bakhshwana*): the girl declares she gives up her right to marry; she's revered as a "bibi" but lives and dies unmarried; leads to hysteria, immorality, even killings. Story of a dying wadera begging forgiveness from his "bibi" daughter, who refused. Author: a grave injustice using the Qur'an.

## Section 2: Sins committed at weddings (p.242–252)
- **Shaving the beard:** haram — changing Allah's creation (An-Nisa 4:119); contradicting the command "differ from the polytheists: let beards grow, trim moustaches" (B 5892, M 259); five wordings (a'fu, awfu, arkhu, arju, waffiru) — Nawawi; imitating disbelievers and women (cursed — B 5886).
- **Plucking eyebrows:** Ibn Mas'ud: Allah cursed women who tattoo, pluck eyebrows/face hair (*namisah*), file teeth for beauty — changing Allah's creation (B 5931, M 2125).
- **Long nails:** against fitrah; limit for trimming nails, moustache, armpit/pubic hair: no more than 40 days (M 258).
- **Gold ring for the groom:** gold is haram for men (T 1720, N).
- **Photography/filming at weddings (p.245–246):** photos of couple and guests (incl. non-mahram women), videos shown to others — immodesty; footage of women dancing has ended up sold on the market. Not allowed.
- **Tight/sheer clothes and unveiling:** "women clothed yet naked… will not enter Paradise nor smell its fragrance" (M 2128).
- **Mixed gatherings (p.246–247):** looking, talking, touching non-mahrams — "zina of the eyes is looking, of the ears listening, tongue speaking, hand touching, feet walking; the heart desires and the private part confirms or denies" (M 2657, B 6243). A woman passing men with perfume is condemned (N) — how much more a decked-out bride among men.

## Section 3: Dowry (jahez) — its Shar'i status and a balanced review (p.248–282)
- Jahez came from long coexistence with Hindus (Hindu *kanyadan*). Two extremes: some treat it as a binding part of marriage; others call any jahez haram/la'nat. Author takes a middle path.
- **Meaning (p.249):** Arabic *jahaz* = to prepare provisions (Yusuf 12:59; "whoever equips a fighter has fought", B 2843). Customary meaning: goods from the bride's side to the groom's home. Greed for jahez is now so strong that girls without it can't marry.
- **Shar'i ruling (p.250–253):** demanding jahez, or making it a condition of marriage (from either side), is **forbidden/invalid**:
  1. Neither the Prophet ﷺ nor Companions ever had such a condition; Sunnah is the model (Al-Ahzab 33:21); "any condition not in Allah's Book is void, even 100 conditions" (B 456).
  2. All household costs and maintenance are the **husband's** duty (An-Nisa 4:34), not the bride's family's. Voluntary help/gifts from a wealthy wife or in-laws are allowed (see later).
  3. It replaces a daughter's **inheritance** — Hindu society denied daughters inheritance and gave dowry instead; Muslims copy this, though Allah fixed her share (An-Nisa 4:11–14).
- **Jahez as a Hindu custom (p.253–254):** Islam's reforms (Al-A'raf 7:157) gave daughters inheritance; jahez is "dan" renamed.
- **Religious harms (p.255–256):** a bid'ah crushing the Sunnah; leads to choosing brides for wealth rather than deen (B 5090 "four reasons"); deprives daughters of inheritance (4:13–14).
- **Social harms (p.256–263):** (1) oppression of poor parents who start saving from a girl's birth (Ghafir 40:31; "I have forbidden oppression for Myself…", M 2577); (2) show-off/display of dowry — riya (Al-Ma'un 107:4–6; "what I fear most for you is minor shirk — riya", Ah); (3) parents resort to bribes, lies, interest (Al-Baqarah 2:188, 2:275–279); (4) girls left unmarried because parents can't afford it (An-Nur 24:32); (5) daughters seen as a burden — like the pre-Islamic attitude (An-Nahl 16:58–59) — and even female foeticide/infanticide (Al-Isra 17:31; "the greatest sins: shirk, then killing your child…", B 6861, M 86); (6) suicides of girls whose parents can't afford dowry (An-Nisa 4:29); (7) brides abused/burnt ("stove-burst" deaths) for "insufficient" dowry (An-Nisa 4:93); (8) people who condemn dowry when marrying daughters but demand it for sons — hypocrisy (Yunus 10:44).
- **Begging for dowry (p.263):** people beg in mosques for daughters' dowry — begging is permitted only for three: one who took on a debt for reconciliation, one struck by calamity, one in severe poverty attested by three wise men; otherwise it's eating haram (Qabisah, M 1044).
- **Medical harms (p.264):** delayed marriage → illnesses (cites a doctor's list: hysteria, epilepsy, etc.); early marriage is healthier (Ar-Rum 30:21).
- **Moral harms (p.264–267):** marriage is required for those able (An-Nisa 4:3; "O young men…", B 5066) and guardians must arrange it (An-Nur 24:32); delaying daughters' marriage for dowry leads to zina (An-Nur 24:2), spreading indecency (24:19), illicit outlets — "zina of the eyes, tongue…" (B 6243, M 2657).
- **Summary of jahez discussion (p.267):** (1) not part of Shari'ah; (2) treating it as a condition of marriage is wrong; (3) demanding it is un-Islamic and immoral; (4) denying inheritance in its place violates Allah's limits; (5) Hindu custom; (6) not found in this form in the Prophet's ﷺ era; (7) compelling it is unlawful; (8–9) countless social, medical, moral harms; (10) its small financial benefit does not outweigh harms.
- **Even India legislated against it (p.268):** several Indian states passed anti-dowry laws (quote from *Muhaddith*, Benares, 1985), yet the custom persists.

### Doubts answered (p.268–277)
- **Is jahez "la'nat" (a curse)?** Calling it la'nat needs proof; there is no text cursing it. But its current form is a Hindu custom and "whoever imitates a people is one of them" (AD 4031). If it isn't a condition/demand, some allowance exists (with conditions below).
- **Is jahez the root of all problems?** No — overstated. It's one social problem among many (politics, economy, education also need Islamic guidance).
- **Did the Prophet ﷺ give dowry to his daughters?** He had four: Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah. No authentic report of dowry for Ruqayyah/Umm Kulthum (both married Uthman, after Abu Lahab's sons divorced them — Uthman "Dhu al-Nurayn"); Uthman was wealthy (equipped the Tabuk army, T 3700). For Zaynab (married Abu al-As), no dowry from the Prophet ﷺ; **Khadijah gave her a necklace** as a gift (later sent as ransom for Abu al-As after Badr) — shows parents may give gifts at marriage, not as custom/show.
- **Did he give Fatimah dowry? (p.271–276):** Nasa'i's chapter "a man equipping his daughter": Ali — the Prophet ﷺ equipped Fatimah with a velvet sheet, a waterskin and a pillow stuffed with idhkhir grass (N 3386; similar Ah, IM 4152). But: Ali had no mahr; the Prophet ﷺ told him to give his Hutami armour as mahr (N 3377, AD 2125); Anas: Ali sold the armour (for ~480 dirhams), the Prophet ﷺ had Bilal buy perfume and household items from that money (*Dala'il al-Nubuwwah* etc.). So the items were bought from **Ali's own mahr money**. Even if from the Prophet ﷺ himself: (1) Ali was his ward/foster-son (raised in his care); (2) Ali was very poor — help is praiseworthy (story of Fatimah asking for a servant; the Prophet ﷺ instead taught tasbih 33/33/34 before sleep — Ah, Ibn Sa'd); Harithah gave them a house; (3) gifts at weddings are recommended ("give gifts, you'll love each other").
- **Conclusions (p.277):** (1) mahr may be demanded in advance, jahez may not be a condition; (2) mahr can be goods instead of cash; (3) mahr is the woman's right; (4) Fatimah's "dowry" was bought from her mahr (most likely); (5) if from the Prophet ﷺ, it was because he was Ali's guardian; (6) helping a poor son-in-law with essentials is good; (7) gifts at weddings recommended; (8) not with intent to show off; (9) gifts don't cancel a daughter's inheritance; (10) demanding jahez is shameful and immoral.
- **Umm Habibah's dowry (p.278–280):** after her husband Ubaydullah b. Jahsh became Christian in Abyssinia, the Prophet ﷺ sent Amr b. Umayyah to the Negus; the Negus married her to him with **4,000 dirhams mahr** and provided her jahez himself (Ah, Hakim); Ibn Sa'd's detailed account: her dream, the maid Abrahah, Khalid b. Sa'id as her agent, 400 dinars, gifts of perfume. Lessons: giving goods at marriage was known among Arabs/Abyssinians; the Prophet's ﷺ silence implies some permissibility — but nothing like today's custom; accepting unrequested gifts is fine, demanding is not.

### Summary and suggestions on jahez (p.280–282)
1–7. As above (not Shar'i; not a condition; inheritance; demanding is a crime; Hindu origin; not in Sunnah; harmful).
8. Government may legally restrict/penalise it.
9. Treating it as a necessary part of marriage makes it a bid'ah.
10. Calling it absolutely la'nat/haram is also extreme.
11. Buying household goods from the mahr money is fine.
12. Helping a poor son-in-law with necessities is fine.
13. Parents may give gifts out of happiness.
14. No show-off/display.
15. No debt, begging, or illicit means to fund it.
16. Don't exceed means.
17. Better to give gifts after the wedding at a suitable time, to avoid resembling the Hindu custom.
18. Prefer **cash** over goods: ends the display; couple buys what they need; avoids extravagance; surplus can go to charity; useful for business.
19. The groom's side should work even harder to end the custom.
20. Moderation, not extremes.

# Chapter 4 — Rights and Duties of Spouses, the Newborn, and In-laws (p.283–378)
Topics list (p.283): conjugal right, inheritance, serving husband, obedience, mahr, guarding his property, talaq, her honour, khul', not leaving home without permission, scolding the wife, voluntary fasting with permission, gratitude, residence, polygamy, justice among wives, good treatment, settling abroad after marriage, urging her to good, maintenance, rights of the newborn, names list, upbringing, mother-in-law & daughter-in-law.

## Section 1: Rights of the husband (p.285–298)
- The husband's right is very great: a woman can't fulfil her Lord's right until she fulfils her husband's; she shouldn't refuse him even on a camel saddle (IM 1853); "If I were to order anyone to prostrate to another, I'd order a wife to prostrate to her husband" (T 1159, IM 1853).
- **Conjugal right:** when he calls her for his need she should come "even if at the oven" (T 1160); angels curse her till morning if she refuses without reason (B 3237, M 1436); some reports: her prayer isn't accepted while he is justly angry (Ibn Khuzaymah).
- **Obedience** (An-Nisa 4:34 — men are qawwam): "If a woman prays her five, fasts Ramadan, guards her chastity and obeys her husband, she may enter Paradise from any gate she wishes" (Ibn Hibban 4163); Husayn b. Mihsan's aunt — "he is your Paradise or your Hell" (Ah, N).
- **Serving the husband/housework (p.288–290):** wife should do the home's cooking, cleaning etc. Fatimah's hands blistered from the hand-mill; asked for a servant; given tasbih instead (B 5361, M 2727). Asma bint Abi Bakr cared for Zubayr's horse, carried date-stones on her head 2 miles, kneaded dough (B 5224, M 2182). Reason: husband bears all expenses; division of roles is fair.
- **Guarding his property (p.290–291):** An-Nisa 4:34 (guarding in absence). "A woman shouldn't spend from her husband's house without permission — not even food? — that is our best wealth" (T 670, IM 2295). Giving charity from his wealth needs his general or specific permission (Nawawi).
- **Not leaving home without permission (p.291–292):** Al-Ahzab 33:33 (stay in your houses; no tabarruj). Ibn Umar: a wife shouldn't go out without permission; if she does, Allah and the angels curse her until she repents/returns (Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi). Ibn Taymiyyah: going out without permission makes her disobedient (nashizah).
- **Guarding her honour (p.292–293):** An-Nur 24:31; Al-Ahzab 33:32 (don't soften speech). "You have a right that they not let anyone you dislike tread your bedding" (M 1218). A woman who attributes a child falsely to a family has no share with Allah (AD 2263).
- **No voluntary fasting without permission (p.294–295):** (B 5192, M 1026). Safwan b. al-Mu'attal's wife complained he beat her for praying/fasting; he explained (long surahs; he was young and couldn't wait; he slept till sunrise due to family work) — the Prophet ﷺ ruled: a wife shouldn't fast voluntarily without permission; one surah suffices; "pray when you wake" (AD 2459).
- **Gratitude to the husband (p.295–296):** "O women, give charity — I saw most of the inhabitants of Hell were women… you curse much and are ungrateful to husbands" (B 304, M 79).
- **Disciplining a disobedient wife (p.296–297):** An-Nisa 4:34: (1) advise, (2) separate beds, (3) light non-injurious hitting — only after the first two fail; "if they obey, seek no way against them". Farewell Sermon: if they commit clear wrong, hit lightly (*ghayr mubarrih*) (T 1163). "Let none of you whip his wife like a slave and then sleep with her at the end of the day" (B 5204, M 2855). Unprovoked hitting is not allowed.
- **Right of talaq:** if the wife's rebellion becomes intolerable, the husband may divorce in the Sunnah way (Ch.6).
- **Polygamy:** he may marry up to four with justice (Ch.10).
- **Inheritance:** husband inherits half of the wife's estate if she has no children, one quarter if she has (An-Nisa 4:12).

## Section 2: Rights of the wife (p.299– )
- Al-Baqarah 2:228 ("they have rights like those against them"); Farewell Sermon: "treat women well, they are like captives with you… you have rights over them and they over you" (T 1163, IM 1851). Abdullah b. Amr fasting daily/praying all night — "your body, eyes and **wife have a right over you**" (B 5199, M 1159).
- Rights will be settled on the Day of Judgment — even the hornless sheep against the horned (M 2582).
- **Conjugal right of the wife (p.300–302):** Amr b. al-As married his son Abdullah to a noble woman; she said he never came to bed due to worship; the Prophet ﷺ told him to fast 3 days a month, finish Qur'an monthly (then allowed every 7 days, Dawud's fast alternate days) — "your wife has a right over you" (B 5052, 5199). Salman and Abu al-Darda': Salman found Umm al-Darda' unkempt as Abu al-Darda' spent all his time in worship; "your Lord, yourself and your family have a right — give each its right"; the Prophet ﷺ: "Salman spoke the truth" (B 1968).
- **Going abroad after marriage (p.302–303):** many men work abroad for years. Umar ruled soldiers shouldn't be away from wives more than **4 months** (some reports 6 — 4 at the front plus travel) (Ibn Taymiyyah *Ahkam al-Zawaj*). Beyond that only for genuine need; if hardship/fitnah, the wife may seek **khul'**.
- **Residence (p.303):** husband provides housing per his means (At-Talaq 65:6). Separate dwellings for co-wives — the Prophet ﷺ gave each wife her own room. Ibn Qudamah: two wives may not be put in one dwelling without their consent.
- **Maintenance (nafaqah) (p.304– ):** all expenses on the husband (Al-Baqarah 2:233 "provision and clothing per custom"; At-Talaq 65:7 per means). Mu'awiyah al-Qushayri: "feed her when you eat, clothe her when you clothe yourself, don't strike the face, don't revile her, don't boycott her except within the house" (AD 2142, IM 1850).
- If the husband is miserly, the wife may take what suffices for her and the children per custom without his knowledge — Hind bint Utbah about Abu Sufyan (B 5364, M 1714).
- **Mahr:** wife's right (An-Nisa 4:24, 4:4); guardians/husband must not seize or pressure her to waive it; she may waive willingly.
- **Good treatment (p.306–307):** An-Nisa 4:19 ("live with them in kindness"). "No believing man should hate a believing woman: if he dislikes one trait he'll be pleased with another" (M 1469). "The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family" (T 3895). Woman created from a rib — if you try to straighten it you'll break it; enjoy her with her crookedness (B 5184, M 1468).
- **Justice between wives (p.307–309):** An-Nisa 4:3 — if you fear injustice, one. "Whoever has two wives and inclines to one will come on the Day of Resurrection with half his body leaning/paralysed" (AD 2133, T 1141, N, IM 1969). Equal spending/residence/turns; children's extra expenses per need. **New wife:** a virgin gets 7 nights, a previously-married woman 3, then turns are divided equally (B 5213, M 1461).
- **Urging her to good (p.309):** At-Tahrim 66:6 ("save yourselves and your families from Fire"); "each of you is a shepherd… the man is shepherd of his household, the woman of her husband's house and children" (B 5200, M 1829).
- **Protecting her honour (p.310):** not exposing her to mixed gatherings; the *dayyuth* (man without ghayrah who tolerates indecency in his family) — "three will not enter Paradise: one disobedient to parents, the dayyuth, and the masculine woman" (Ah, N, Hakim).
- **Right of khul' (p.310–312):** if the wife has a reasonable aversion, she can ask for separation by returning the mahr; if the husband refuses, she goes to court, which can order or annul the nikah. Al-Baqarah 2:229. **Wife of Thabit b. Qays:** "I don't fault his religion or character but fear ingratitude (kufr) in Islam" — "will you return his garden?" — "yes" — "take the garden and give one talaq" (B 5273). Her iddah: **one menstrual cycle** (AD 2229, T 1185).
- **Khul' — talaq or annulment? (p.312):** author's stronger view: **faskh (annulment)**, not talaq: (1) no right of return; (2) it's the woman's initiative; (3) she may remarry the same husband with a new nikah; (4) iddah = one period; (5) can occur via talaq or via annulment. Warning: "Any woman who asks her husband for divorce without a reason — the scent of Paradise is forbidden to her" (AD 2226, T 1187, IM 2055).
- **Inheritance (p.313):** wife gets **1/4** of husband's estate if no children, **1/8** if there are children (An-Nisa 4:12).

## Section 3: Rights of the newborn (p.314– )
### Rejoicing at birth of boy or girl (p.314–316)
- Being sad at a girl's birth, even divorcing the wife for it, is wrong: (1) Allah grants sons, daughters, both, or barrenness (Ash-Shura 42:49–50); (2) pre-Islamic Arabs grieved at daughters (An-Nahl 16:58–59); (3) we don't know which is better — Imran's wife hoped for a son but got Maryam, mother of Isa (Al Imran 3:35–36).
- Infertility: often due to the man, not the woman — seek medical treatment; avoid amulets (shirk); ruqyah from righteous people is OK (author's book on jinn/magic referenced). A man wanting children may marry a second wife, but divorcing the first is wrong; the first wife should be accommodating.

### Adhan in the newborn's ear (p.317–326)
- Practised for centuries across the Ummah. Recently **Mufti Mubashir Ahmad Rabbani** (Jama'at al-Da'wah, *Majallat al-Da'wah* June 2002) argued it is not Sunnah:
  - Abu Rafi': "I saw the Prophet ﷺ give adhan in Hasan's ear when Fatimah gave birth" (T 1514, AD, Ah, Hakim) — narrator Asim b. Ubaydullah is weak (Ibn Hajar).
  - Ibn Abbas's report (adhan right, iqamah left) — fabricated chain (Muhammad b. Yunus al-Kudaymi, a forger).
  - Husayn b. Ali: "whoever gives adhan in the right ear and iqamah in the left, Umm al-Sibyan won't harm him" (Bayhaqi *Shu'ab*) — Yahya b. al-Ala' al-Razi, a liar.
- **Response** (*Da'wat Ahl-e-Hadith*, 2002): Asim is weak but not abandoned (Ijli: "no harm in him"; Ibn Adi: his hadith is written); Abu Rafi's report is strengthened as hasan li-ghayrihi (Albani at one point treated Abu Rafi's report as supported (*Silsilah Da'ifah* 321); other scholars also graded it hasan); Tirmidhi: "practice is upon it". Umar b. Abd al-Aziz did adhan/iqamah for his newborns (Abd al-Razzaq). Baghawi, Ibn Qudamah (*al-Mughni*: recommended for father), Nawawi (*al-Majmu'*: Sunnah — adhan right, iqamah left), Ibn al-Qayyim (*Tuhfat al-Mawdud*: wisdom — first words heard are Allah's greatness and tawhid; Shaytan flees), Sayyid Sabiq (*Fiqh al-Sunnah*), Wahbah al-Zuhayli (*al-Fiqh al-Islami*).
- **Author's preferred view (p.324):** giving adhan is **permissible/Sunnah** and safer — centuries of Ummah consensus in practice ("my Ummah will not agree on error", T); even if the reports are weak, they are supported by ijma'.

### Tahneek (p.325)
- Sunnah: chew a date and put a little in the newborn's mouth, with du'a. Anas took Abu Talhah's son to the Prophet ﷺ, who chewed dates, did tahneek and named him Abdullah (B 5470, M 2144). Abu Musa's son — named Ibrahim, tahneek, du'a for blessing (B 5467, M 2145).

### Circumcision (p.325)
- For boys, as early as possible. "Five are from fitrah: circumcision, shaving pubic hair, plucking armpit hair, trimming nails, trimming moustache" (B 5889).

### When to name the child (p.326–328)
- Two sets of reports: naming on the day of birth (Anas/Abdullah b. Abi Talhah; Abu Musa/Ibrahim; Asma bint Abi Bakr's son Abdullah b. al-Zubayr at Quba (B, M); Abu Usayd's son al-Mundhir (B, M)) and on the 7th day (Amr b. Shu'ayb — name, remove harm, aqiqah on the 7th, T 2832; Samurah — "every child is in pledge for his aqiqah, slaughtered on the 7th day, head shaved, named", AD 2838, T 1522).
- Reconciliation: if no aqiqah is planned, name on day 1; else on day 7 — or any time within 7 days (Ibn al-Qayyim, *Tuhfat al-Mawdud*: wide latitude). A kunyah (Abu/Umm so-and-so) may be given even before marriage/children (M).

### Good names (p.328–329)
- Choose names with good meaning (Muhsin, Hamid, Abid; for girls with "ah" ending). Change bad names: Umar's daughter Asiyah ("disobedient") renamed **Jamilah** (M 2139). Prefer names of prophets and righteous people. **Forbidden names:** those implying shirk — Nabi Bakhsh, Pir Bakhsh, Piran Ditta, Abd al-Nabi, Ghulam Nabi, Abd al-Rasul; also names based on numerology.
- **Most beloved names to Allah: Abdullah and Abd al-Rahman** (M 2132).

### Name lists (p.330–339) — from Maulana Faruq Asghar Sarim's *Pyare Naam*
- Tables (name + Urdu meaning) of: names of prophets (Adam, Nuh, Idris, Salih, Hud, Ibrahim, Lut, Ishaq, Ya'qub, Yusuf, Khidr, Shu'ayb, Musa, Harun, Yusha', Ilyas, Yasa', Dawud, Sulayman, Ayyub, Yunus, Dhul-Kifl, Uzayr, Luqman, Zakariyya, Yahya, Isa, Muhammad); descriptive names of the Prophet ﷺ (Ahmad, Amin, Bashir, Jawad, Hamid, Habib, Khalil, Khatib, Rashid, Siraj, Shafi', Shahid, Sadiq, Siddiq, Tahir, Adil, Aqib, Kalim, Qasim, Mubashshir, Mujtaba, Mahmud, Mudaththir, Murtada, Muzzammil, Mustafa, Misbah, Mansur, Munir, Nadhir, Nasir…); names of the Prophet's ﷺ wives (Khadijah, A'ishah, Hafsah, Zaynab, Juwayriyah, Ramlah/Umm Habibah, Sawdah, Safiyyah, Maymunah, Hind/Umm Salamah); his children and grandchildren (Ibrahim, Abdullah, Qasim, Hasan, Husayn, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah, Umamah); ~150 names of famous Companions (Aban, Abu Dharr, Abu Hurayrah, Arqam, Usamah, As'ad, Anas, Uways, Tamim, Jabir, Jarir, Hudhayfah, Hassan, Hamzah, Khalid, Khabbab, Rafi', Zubayr, Salman, Suhayl, Sa'd, Ammar, Abbad, Ubaydullah, Ikrimah, Amr, Fudayl, Qatadah, Ka'b, Mus'ab, Mu'adh, Mughirah, Nu'man, Walid, etc.); and ~130 names of female Companions (Asma, Umaymah, Aminah, Barirah, Thuwaybah, Habibah, Halimah, Hamidah, Khalidah, Khansa, Khawlah, Rabi'ah, Rufaydah, Ruqayqah, Zubayrah, Sakinah, Sumayyah, Salamah, Shifa, Shayma, Tahirah, Aliyah, Atiyyah, Afra, Aqilah, Ghazalah, Fadilah, Kabshah, Laylah, Lubabah, Marjanah, Mutia, Nafisah, Nusaybah, Waraqah, etc.).

### Aqiqah (p.340–344)
- **Obligatory or Sunnah?** Zahiris, Hasan al-Basri, Layth and others: obligatory; Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Aisha, Malik, Shafi'i, Ahmad and majority: emphasised Sunnah; Abu Hanifah/Hanafis: merely permissible (some call it a pre-Islamic custom). **Author:** whoever can afford it must do it — two animals for a boy, one for a girl; if only one is affordable for a boy, one suffices; if unable, do it later when able.
- **Number:** "for a boy two equivalent sheep/goats, for a girl one" (AD 2842, Amr b. Shu'ayb); Umm Kurz (AD 2834, T 1516, N 4217); Aisha (T 1513). The Prophet ﷺ did one ram each for Hasan and Husayn (AD 2841, Ibn Abbas); Nasa'i: two rams each (N 4224). Ibn Hajar: two for a boy is recommended, not a condition. Ibn Umar and Urwah did one per child, boy or girl.
- **Animal:** goat or sheep, male or female ("whether male or female doesn't harm", Umm Kurz). Cow/camel not established — Aisha refused a camel for Abd al-Rahman's son: "two equal sheep as the Prophet ﷺ said" (Tahawi). Some Malikis allow any qurbani animal; author: closer to Sunnah is sheep/goat; sharing aqiqah in a qurbani cow/camel is against Sunnah. Age conditions of qurbani not required but preferable (Hanafi/Shafi'i view requires them).
- **Time:** 7th day; else 14th or 21st (Buraydah — Bayhaqi; Hakim from Aisha — weak per Albani); if missed, whenever able.

### Upbringing and education of children (p.344– )
- Parents are responsible for children's education/upbringing until adulthood and for marrying them; treat sons and daughters equally.
- Aisha gave a poor woman with two daughters three dates; she gave each girl one and split hers between them; the Prophet ﷺ: "Allah has made Paradise obligatory for her because of this" (IM 3668; M 2630).
- "Whoever has three daughters and is patient with them, feeds, gives drink and clothes them from his means, they'll be a shield from the Fire" (IM 3669, Uqbah b. Amir); two daughters well treated → Paradise (IM 3670, Ibn Abbas); "Whoever raises two girls until maturity will come on the Day of Resurrection like this with me" (joining fingers) (M 2631, Anas).

### Selected article on raising children (p.346–355) — from Muhammad Jamil Zainu's *Tarbiyat-e-Awlad ke Islami Usul* (tr. Hafiz Khalid)
- At-Tahrim 66:6; "each of you is a shepherd" (B, M); guiding one person is better than red camels (B, M); a righteous child praying for parents is among three deeds that continue after death (M). Parents/teachers must model the behaviour.
- **Faith:** teach the kalimah and its meaning early; love of Allah; desire for Paradise via prayer, fasting, obeying parents; fear of Hell for neglecting prayer, disobedience, lying, interest, theft; "When you ask, ask Allah; when you seek help, seek Allah's help" (T).
- **Prayer & Qur'an:** "Teach your children prayer at 7, discipline them for it at 10, and separate their beds" (Ah, AD); parents pray in front of children, take them to the mosque, keep a book on salah; teach Fatihah, short surahs, tashahhud; hire a tutor for tajwid/hifz; Friday prayer; handle mistakes gently, not harshly; accustom them to fasting from age 7.
- **Avoiding haram:** stop swearing, cursing, idle talk; explain harm of gambling (lottery, carrom etc. for money); keep them from obscene literature, nude pictures, spy novels, cinema, TV/films; explain harms of smoking (cancer, etc.) — it is haram; teach truthfulness, no "joking lies" — "signs of a hypocrite: lies when speaks, breaks promises, betrays trust" (B, M); feed them halal only (no bribes, interest, theft); don't curse your children — say "Aslahakallah" (may Allah reform you); warn of shirk — calling on other than Allah (Yunus 10:106).
- **Hijab and covering:** girls should wear covering clothes from childhood; no tight trousers/short dresses; teach headscarf from ~7; at puberty full hijab/jilbab (Al-Ahzab 33:59, 33:33). Boys and girls must not imitate the opposite sex — "Allah cursed men who imitate women and women who imitate men" (B); "whoever imitates a people is one of them" (AD).
- **Manners:** use the right hand for giving/taking, eating, drinking, writing; say Bismillah and Alhamdulillah; eat sitting; cleanliness — trim nails, wash hands before/after eating, istinja and toilet hygiene; correct children privately and gently; boycotting more than three days not allowed; repeat the adhan and say the du'a after it (B); separate beds/rooms for boys and girls if possible; remove harmful objects from the path; avoid bad company; greet with "Assalamu alaykum wa rahmatullah"; kindness to neighbours; honour guests.

## Section 4: Mother-in-law and daughter-in-law — rights and duties (p.356–378)
(Adapted mainly from Muhammad Abdul Hafeez's book *Saas aur Bahu*, which the author praises.)
- Historically the mother-in-law dominated; with modernity, the daughter-in-law increasingly dominates. Islam offers the middle way: love, sacrifice, forgiveness, respect for elders, kindness to the young.
- **Our predicament (p.357–358):** subcontinent Muslims kept Hindu customs (joint family, dowry, etc.). Typical cycle: the mother-in-law looks for faults; the bride is lavishly courted during engagement then criticised; small matters snowball into jealousy, backbiting, insults; the son is caught between mother and wife; demand for a separate house follows.
- **Three solutions (p.359–363):**
  1. **Seek Allah's pleasure** — answer anger with love, harshness with gentleness, evil with good; prefer others over oneself (Al-Hashr 59:9).
  2. **Want for others what you want for yourself** — "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself" (B, M).
  3. **Joint family system (p.360–363):** it is a custom (urf), not a religious requirement. A woman has a right to her own home where she's in charge: Al-Ahzab 33:33, At-Talaq 65:6; "the woman is shepherd over her husband's house" (B). Joint living causes problems: lack of privacy for spouses; hijab from brothers-in-law etc. becomes impossible; wife must please many people. Arguments for joint living (unity, savings, support) — but separate living isn't "Western" fragmentation (that came from rejecting religion). Cost argument: people spend lakhs on wedding customs yet say they can't afford a house; the Prophet's ﷺ houses were single rooms. The Prophet ﷺ gave each wife a separate dwelling and gave Ali and Fatimah their own house.
- **Real aim:** not to promote separate living but to apply Islamic etiquette (consent, hijab, rights of parents/relatives, children's upbringing) — each family can choose what works.
- **Principle for all:** "each of you is a shepherd" (M).
- **Bride's parents' duties (p.364):** give daughters religious upbringing; teach rights, sacrifice, respect, good manners; teach her that husband's obedience (except in sin) is obligatory; treat his relatives well; in-laws are like her own parents; husband's siblings like her own. Many parents neglect religious training while spending on worldly education; TV, VCR, films, magazines corrupt.
- Parents often choose a groom by wealth/degree/looks instead of deen (the "four reasons" hadith). Bride's parents should not encourage her to dominate in-laws or run home with complaints; the Prophet ﷺ sent Fatimah back to Ali after a complaint, reconciling them.
- **Mother-in-law's duties (p.365–367):** raise her son religiously, teach him to marry for deen; accept that his attention shifts to his wife; treat the bride like a daughter, overlook faults, never taunt about dowry; don't interfere in the couple's private matters; protect hijab arrangements (keep sons/men from casual contact with bride); don't overburden her with housework; don't speak against the bride to her son; the wife is a trust (An-Nisa 4:58).
- **Son's duties (p.367–371):** as **son** — honour parents (Luqman 31:14; "Allah has forbidden disobedience to mothers", B); don't make wife do all service to parents that you should do yourself; "the best kindness after a father's death is keeping ties with his friends" (M); as **husband** — provide a peaceful home; don't overburden wife with serving all in-laws; care for her in illness, menses, childbirth; let her visit her parents; protect her from in-law men: "the brother-in-law is death" (B); give her pocket money; teach religion; support her and children; balance rights of parents and wife.
- **Daughter-in-law's duties (p.371–376):** she is entering her husband's home as partner; respect the mother who raised him; obey husband — Asma bint Yazid: men get reward of jihad/Jumu'ah etc.; "a wife's good companionship of her husband equals all that"; mother's right is thrice the father's; "the greatest sin after shirk is disobedience to parents" — she should help her husband honour his parents (Al-Isra 17:23 "don't say uff"); don't abandon children to mother-in-law's care; don't leave home for jobs/social work shifting burdens onto the elderly; elderly in-laws should not be neglected — "He is not of us who does not respect our elders and show mercy to our young" (T); Abu Bakr and Umar served an old blind woman; in-laws' rights: (1) "a woman is shepherd over her husband's house" (B) — includes his parents; (2) Al-Isra 17:26 (give relatives their right); (3) "whoever doesn't thank people doesn't thank Allah" (Ah, T); keep good ties even if living apart — "rahim is derived from al-Rahman: whoever joins it, Allah joins him" (B); "whoever wants increased provision and life, let him keep ties of kinship" (B *Adab*).
- Cut waste of time/money (shopping, excess decoration, unnecessary functions, TV, novels, cricket, chess, cards) and spend on others' rights instead (p.376).

# Chapter 5 — Forbidden, Corrupt (fasid) and Void Marriages (p.377–396)
Topics: mut'ah, halala, shighar.

## Section 1: Nikah al-Mut'ah (temporary marriage) (p.379–381)
- Definition: marriage for a fixed period (hours/days) for payment; ends automatically. Allowed early in Islam in limited circumstances (travel/war — Hazimi says only in travel, *al-I'tibar*), then **permanently prohibited** at the conquest of Makkah. Some Companions didn't hear of the prohibition until Umar enforced it.
- **Evidence:** (1) Al-Mu'minun 23:5–6: lawful only with wives or slave-women — a mut'ah woman is neither (no inheritance, maintenance, divorce, iddah); Ibn Abbas: this verse abrogated mut'ah; "every other private part is haram" (T 1122). Shia claim Ibn Abbas allowed it — he retracted (Jassas). (2) Ali: the Prophet ﷺ forbade mut'ah and domestic donkey meat at Khaybar (B 4216, M 1407). (3) Sabrah al-Juhani: "I had permitted mut'ah; Allah has now forbidden it until the Day of Resurrection; whoever has such a woman, let her go, and don't take back what you gave" (M 1406). (4) Jabir: practised in Abu Bakr's time and early Umar's until Umar forbade it (M 1405). (5) Umar: the Prophet ﷺ permitted it thrice then forbade it; anyone doing it while married would be stoned (IM 1963). (6) **Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq**: "it is zina itself" (Bayhaqi).

## Section 2: Nikah al-Halala (p.382–392)
- After three talaqs (in separate sittings), the wife is unlawful for the husband unless she genuinely marries another man who later divorces her or dies (Al-Baqarah 2:230). Marrying her with the intention/condition of divorcing after one night so she becomes lawful for the first husband = **halala** — cursed and haram (definition from Ibn al-Athir's *al-Nihayah*).
- Evidence: Ibn Mas'ud — "Allah's Messenger cursed the muhallil and the muhallal lahu" (T 1120, N, AD 2076); Jabir and Ali (T 1119); Uqbah b. Amir — "Shall I tell you of the borrowed billy-goat? The muhallil; Allah cursed the muhallil and muhallal lahu" (IM 1936, Hakim); Ibn Abbas (IM 1934); Ibn Umar: a man married a woman to make her lawful for his brother — "No, only a marriage of desire; we considered this zina (*sifah*) in the Prophet's ﷺ time" (Hakim, Bayhaqi); Uthman separated such a couple — "she returns only through a marriage of desire, not deception" (Bayhaqi); Umar: "any muhallil and muhallal brought to me, I will stone them both" (Abd al-Razzaq, Bayhaqi).
- More: Ibn Umar — those doing halala remain fornicators even if together 20 years if Allah knows they intended halala (Hakim); Ibn Abbas — "whoever tries to deceive Allah, Allah will deceive him" (Bayhaqi); Hasan al-Basri & Ibrahim al-Nakha'i — if any of the three (husband, wife, muhallil) intended halala, the nikah is invalid and doesn't make her lawful (Ibn Hazm *al-Muhalla*).
- Some fuqaha said halala is sinful but still makes her lawful for the first husband; today some even offer halala as a "service". Author: clear texts make it haram; avoid all such tricks.
- **How to avoid halala (p.386–387):** the need arises because many count **three talaqs in one sitting as three**. Author (Ahl-e-Hadith view): by Qur'an & Sunnah, three in one sitting = **one** (revocable) talaq; follow that and halala is never needed (details in Ch.6).

## Section 3: Nikah al-Shighar (exchange marriage / watta-satta) (p.388–393)
- Definition: marrying off one's daughter/sister to someone on the condition that he marries his daughter/sister to you. Bukhari/Muslim (Ibn Umar): "the Prophet ﷺ forbade shighar — a man marrying his daughter to another on condition the other marries him his daughter, **with no mahr between them**" (B 5112, M 1415).
- **Dispute:** is "no mahr" an essential part of the definition or incidental? Is the definition from the Prophet ﷺ or a narrator? Fuqaha agree an exchange with no mahr is haram; differ if mahr is fixed: Malik, Ibn Hazm — still void; Shafi'is, Hanafis, Hanbalis — valid if mahr al-mithl is fixed. Ibn Rushd explains the root of disagreement.
- **Author's preferred view:** shighar is prohibited **whether or not mahr is fixed**, because the essence is the *condition* of exchange. Strongest proof: Abbas b. Abdullah married his daughter to Abd al-Rahman b. al-Hakam, who married his daughter to him, **with mahr** fixed; **Mu'awiyah** ordered Marwan to separate them: "this is the shighar the Prophet ﷺ forbade" (AD 2075, Ah) — no Companion objected (Ibn Hazm). Tirmidhi: shighar is annulled even with mahr — view of Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ishaq. The "no mahr" definition is likely from **Nafi'** (narrator) — Bukhari has Nafi' explaining it (B 6960); Shafi'i was unsure whether it was from the Prophet ﷺ, Ibn Umar, Nafi' or Malik.
- **Hadith on prohibition:** Ibn Umar (B 5112, M 1415); Abu Hurayrah — "shighar is: marry me your daughter and I'll marry you mine, or your sister and I'll marry you mine" (M 1416); "there is no shighar in Islam" (M 1415, N 3337).
- **Exception (p.393):** if there is no condition — two families independently and freely marry into each other — it is allowed. Fatwa of Hafiz Abd al-Mannan Nurpuri: an Ahl-e-Hadith man's daughter married into a family; three years later that family's daughter is proposed for his son without any prior condition — not shighar (*Ahkam wa Masa'il*).

# Chapter 6 — Talaq (Divorce) and Iddah (p.395–430)
Topics: status and method of talaq; talaq under compulsion, intoxication, insanity; written and phone talaq; divorce at parents' order; iddah; mourning; missing husband.

## Section 1: Status and method of talaq (p.397– )
- **Discouraging divorce:** marriage is a covenant; talaq/khul' exist only when living together becomes impossible. Laqit b. Sabirah complained of his wife's foul tongue — "divorce her"; he said they had a child/companionship — "then advise her; if there's good in her she'll listen" (AD 142, Ah). Hindu law has no divorce at all; Islam allows exit for valid reasons but dislikes it without reason.
- "Any woman who asks for divorce without reason, the scent of Paradise is forbidden" (T 1187, AD 2226, IM 2055); "a woman seeking khul' without reason won't smell Paradise" (T 1186); "the greatest sin with Allah is a man marrying a woman, fulfilling his need, then divorcing her and taking her mahr" (Hakim; Albani *Sahihah* 999).
- **Sunnah method (p.399):** not in haste; after reflection, before two upright witnesses, **one talaq** while she is in a state of purity (tuhr) in which they haven't had intercourse, or while she is pregnant. Talaq during menses or in a tuhr with intercourse is non-Sunnah (bid'ah). Ibn Umar divorced during menses — the Prophet ﷺ told Umar: "order him to take her back, then divorce her when pure or pregnant" (M 1471, AD 2183); "if he wishes, he may divorce her before touching her" (B 5251). Witnesses are commanded (At-Talaq 65:2) but talaq occurs even without them (precaution recommended). Talaq occurs with clear words or with kinayah (indirect words) if intended — avoid such words when angry.
- **During iddah (p.400–402):** after a first or second (revocable) talaq the wife stays in the husband's house; he provides housing and maintenance (At-Talaq 65:1–2; don't expel them; on completion retain kindly or part kindly with two witnesses). Wisdom: chance of reconciliation. In Pakistan she's often sent to her parents immediately — contrary to the Qur'an. After the **third** talaq no housing/maintenance — Fatimah bint Qays: "no maintenance or housing for you" (M 1480, AD 2284, T 1135) — **unless pregnant** (At-Talaq 65:6 — maintain until delivery).
- **Return after first talaq (p.402):** during iddah he may take her back (rujū') without new nikah (Al-Baqarah 2:228). After iddah ends, they may remarry with a new nikah; guardians must not prevent it (2:232).
- **Second talaq (p.402–403):** same procedure. It can also be given before the first iddah ends without rujū'. Hafiz Abd al-Mannan Nurpuri: "talaq is twice" (2:229) covers both cases; no fixed gap is required, but 2 or 3 at once count as one. During the second iddah, housing/maintenance still the husband's.
- **Third talaq (p.403–404):** after the second (with rujū'/remarriage), a third talaq makes her unlawful until she marries another man genuinely (not for halala) and he divorces her or dies; then the first husband may remarry her if both agree (2:230).
- **Written and phone talaq (p.404– ):** talaq occurs whether spoken or written, face-to-face or by phone, whether or not she receives the letter. But a mere intention/thought without speaking or writing does not count: "Allah has forgiven my Ummah what crosses their minds as long as they don't act or speak" (B, M).
- Once expressed verbally or in writing, talaq occurs — even if said jokingly, deceitfully, or with/without the wife hearing. Exception: talaq under intoxication, insanity or compulsion does not count.

### Talaq under compulsion, intoxication or insanity (p.405–406)
- If relatives/others force a husband by violence/threat to divorce, it does **not** take effect: "No talaq or emancipation under *ighlaq*" (AD 2193, IM 2046, Ah, Hakim) — ighlaq = compulsion (Abu Ubayd, Ibn Qutaybah etc.). "Allah has pardoned my Ummah mistakes, forgetfulness and what they're forced into" (IM 2043–45). Ibn Abbas: talaq of the drunk and the coerced is not valid (B, ta'liq). Ibn Qudamah lists Umar, Ali, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr, Jabir, Shurayh, Ata', Tawus, Umar b. Abd al-Aziz etc. as holding coerced talaq invalid.

### Three talaqs in one sitting (p.406–409)
- Three talaqs said/written at once count as **one revocable** talaq:
  1. Ibn Abbas: in the Prophet's ﷺ time, Abu Bakr's, and the first two years of Umar's caliphate, three talaqs were counted as one; Umar then said people are hastening in something where they had latitude — "let us enforce it on them" — and did so (M 1472, Ah, AD 2200). Author: this was an **administrative/punitive measure** by Umar, not Shari'ah; reportedly Umar later regretted it (Ibn al-Qayyim, *Ighathat al-Lahfan*).
  2. **Rukanah b. Abd Yazid** divorced his wife thrice in one sitting and grieved; the Prophet ﷺ: "in one sitting? That is one; take her back if you wish" — and he did (Ah 1/265, AD, Ibn Hajar strengthened).
- Wisdom: pre-Islamic men divorced and took back endlessly; Islam limited it to three, with reflection between. Counting three-at-once as three is unjust, as angry men often say it a thousand times and regret later.
- Those (Hanafis etc.) who count them as three force people toward halala. Following the closer-to-Sunnah view avoids halala and saves homes.

## Section 2: Divorcing the wife at parents' command (p.410–417)
- Divorce without reason is disliked; seeking it without reason is a major sin. Principle: "no obedience to a creature in disobedience of the Creator" (Ah); "obedience is only in what is right" (B 7145, M 1840); Luqman 31:15 (don't obey parents in shirk, but treat them kindly).
- Rule: if parents' demand is based on a **reasonable cause** (e.g. the wife's bad conduct/religion), the son should comply; if it is based on whim/spite, it need not be obeyed — and they should be advised against it.
- **Ibrahim and Isma'il's wives** (B 3364): Ibrahim visited when Isma'il was away; the first wife complained of hardship and didn't host him; he left the message "change your doorstep" → Isma'il divorced her. The second wife praised Allah and hosted him → "keep your doorstep". Shows the father's advice was based on a valid reason (her ingratitude/bad manners) and he confirmed the good wife.
- **Ibn Umar's wife:** Umar disliked a wife Ibn Umar loved; the Prophet ﷺ told him "divorce her" / "obey your father" (AD 5138, T 1189, IM 2088, Ah). Author: Umar had a valid reason (Ahmad's report: "Abdullah has a wife I dislike for him"); Sa'ati (*al-Fath al-Rabbani*): Umar disliked her for something unsuitable and was "inspired" (mulham); Sindhi: obeying parents takes precedence over desire when their command aligns with religion.
- **Mulla Ali Qari:** a son isn't obliged to divorce at parents' order even if they're hurt, since harm to the son may be great. Author: this is the other extreme.
- **Ibn al-Arabi & al-Mundhiri's verdict (p.417):** a dutiful son loves what his father loves and dislikes what he dislikes when the father is wise/insightful; if the father's dislike is mere whim, it's recommended (not required) to comply. Author agrees: if the demand rests on a reasonable cause, obey; otherwise not obligatory.

## Section 3: Wife of a missing husband (mafqud) (p.418–422)
- No explicit Qur'an/hadith text; Companions' rulings: she waits **4 years**, then observes the iddah of death (**4 months 10 days**), then may remarry. Umar (Muwatta, Abd al-Razzaq, Bayhaqi); Umar and Uthman ruled the same (Abd al-Razzaq); Ibn Abbas and Ibn Umar (Bayhaqi); Ibn Hajar: Umar, Uthman, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Ibn Mas'ud (one report), Nakha'i, Ata', Zuhri, Makhul, Sha'bi held this; most agree the 4 years start from when the case reaches the court.
- **Bukhari's view:** wait **one year** (analogy with lost property — announce for a year). Author: the Companions' view is stronger generally; but where the woman faces hardship (chastity, maintenance), a court ruling after one year may be allowed.
- **Hanafi view:** wait until 90/100/120 years of age — impractical; modern Hanafis (Ashraf Ali Thanawi, *Bahishti Zewar*) give fatwa on Maliki view (4 years) due to necessity.
- **If the first husband returns after she remarried (p.421–422):** Malik — first husband has no claim (in a later view: if she hasn't consummated with the second he has more right). Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin: first husband may choose to let the second marriage stand or take her back (after iddah from the second). Ibn Hajar: fuqaha agree he may choose between taking her back or taking back his mahr; Ali: she remains the first husband's wife whether or not the second consummated (Fath al-Bari). Author: first husband retains the right.

## Section 4: Rulings on iddah (p.423–428)
- Iddah = waiting period after separation; no engagement or marriage during it.
  1. **Divorced woman who menstruates:** three *quru'* (menstrual cycles) (Al-Baqarah 2:228).
  2. **Woman not menstruating** (old age or young age): **3 months** (At-Talaq 65:4).
  3. **Pregnant divorcee:** until delivery (At-Talaq 65:4).
  4. **Widow:** **4 months 10 days** (Al-Baqarah 2:234).
  5. **Wife of missing husband:** 4 years wait then 4 months 10 days (above).
  6. **Khul':** one menstrual cycle — Rubayyi' bint Mu'awwidh was ordered by the Prophet ﷺ (T, AD, N); if she doesn't menstruate, one month (analogy).
  7. **Divorced before consummation/valid seclusion:** no iddah (Al-Ahzab 33:49). But if the husband **dies** before consummation, she observes 4 months 10 days (Birwa' case).
  8. **Slave-woman:** two menstrual cycles; if she doesn't menstruate, 1½–2 months (Ibn Rushd, *Bidayat al-Mujtahid*).
  9. If, during the iddah of a revocable talaq, the husband gives a second talaq, the first iddah ends and a new one starts; if the husband dies during a revocable iddah, she switches to the widow's iddah (4m 10d) (Abu Bakr al-Jaza'iri, *Minhaj al-Muslim*).
  10. If during iddah by menstrual cycles/months she turns out pregnant, the iddah becomes until delivery.

## Section 5: Mourning (sog/ihdad) (p.426–427)
- A widow mourns during her iddah: no attractive clothes, jewellery, adornment, henna, kohl, perfume. "It isn't lawful for a believing woman to mourn for anyone more than three days, except for her husband — four months and ten days" (B 5342, M 938, Umm Habibah). Umm Atiyyah: no kohl, perfume, dyed clothes except '*asb* (plain woven); after ghusl from menses she may use a little qust/azfar for odour (B 5341, M 938).
- She should stay in her husband's house for the iddah, going out only for essential needs by day and returning by night. **Furay'ah bint Malik**: wanted to move to her family after her husband was killed — "stay in your house until the term is fulfilled" (AD 2300, T 1204, N, IM 2031, Muwatta). Divorced women likewise. If the house is unsafe for her honour/life, she may move to relatives (Saudi Permanent Committee fatwa).

# Chapter 7 — Miscellaneous and Complex Marriage Issues (p.429–452)
Topics: child marriage; delay between nikah and rukhsati / intimacy before rukhsati; marrying someone much older/younger; option after puberty to annul; Pakistani law restricting child marriage; marriage of fornicators; marriage during pregnancy; marrying a fornicatress.

## Section 1: Child marriage (p.431–435)
- In the Prophet's ﷺ time, guardians married off children; rukhsati was usually after puberty. Islam kept this with the safeguard that the child may annul after maturity if unhappy.
- **Evidence:** At-Talaq 65:4 — iddah of "those who have not yet menstruated" is 3 months → implies marriage and divorce of pre-pubescent girls is possible (Ibn Kathir; Maududi, *Tafhim al-Qur'an*: Qur'an treats it as permissible — no Muslim may forbid it). Aisha: the Prophet ﷺ married her at 6 and consummated at 9 (B 5134, M 1422). Ibn Hajar (via Muhallab): consensus that a father may marry off his young virgin daughter even if not yet ready for intercourse.
- **Option after puberty:** if a child married in childhood is unhappy after puberty, she may have the nikah annulled through court; a boy may give talaq. Ibn Abbas: a virgin girl came saying her father married her against her will — the Prophet ﷺ gave her the choice (AD 2096, IM 1875).
- **Pakistani law (the book cites a 1991 family-law provision, sec. 12)** restricts child marriage (minimum 18 boys / 16 girls) with fines/jail. Author: contradicts Shari'ah; scholars should review it.
- **Wisdom of permitting it** — Maulana Muhammad Taqi Usmani (former judge, Federal Shariat Court, *Hamare Aa'ili Masa'il*): (1) a father sees a child going astray and has a good match available; (2) a terminally ill father wants to secure his daughter's future before death; (3) a poor widow with a daughter, at risk of exploitation, finds a decent match; (4) a peasant fears his landlord's predatory intent toward his daughter; (5) the law will increase zina and abductions.

## Section 2: Other complex issues (p.436–438)
- **Gap between nikah and rukhsati; intimacy before rukhsati:** a gap is allowed (Aisha — nikah at 6, rukhsati at 9 — B 5133). After a valid nikah, intimacy before rukhsati is not sinful in principle (they're husband and wife), but socially frowned upon and may expose them to suspicion; if the girl is very young it should be avoided for medical reasons.
- **Marrying someone much older or younger:** no Shar'i problem. Ibn Baz: young women shouldn't refuse an older man who is righteous; the Prophet ﷺ was 53 and Aisha 9 when the marriage was consummated; Khadijah was 40 and he 25. What matters is religion and suitability (*Fatawa for women*, Darussalam).

## Section 3: Marriage of those who committed zina (p.439– )
- **Ruling on zina:** major sin with hadd punishment if proved by four eyewitnesses or confession before a court: 100 lashes for unmarried (An-Nur 24:2) + one year exile (B 6828); stoning for married (B 6830, M 1691). A pregnant woman is not punished until delivery (M 1695).
- **Marriage of a man and woman who committed zina with each other (p.440–442):** if punished by the court, the punishment is expiation ("whoever commits any of these and is punished, it's an expiation", B, M 1709); then they may marry. If not reported, they should conceal their sin ("whoever conceals a Muslim in this world, Allah will conceal him on the Day of Resurrection", M 2590) and repent sincerely; then they may marry each other: "One who repents from sin is like one who has no sin" (IM 4250). Ibn Abbas: a man had a haram relationship with a woman, then both repented and wanted to marry — "its beginning was fornication, its end is marriage; there's no harm" (Ibn Kathir). If the matter reaches court, the hadd/ta'zir is applied (Umar, Abu Bakr cases — Abd al-Razzaq, Bayhaqi, Ibn Abi Shaybah).
- **Marriage during pregnancy from zina (p.442–444):** Malikis, Hanbalis, Abu Yusuf: a woman pregnant from zina cannot be married — by the fornicator or anyone else — until she delivers: "no intercourse with a pregnant woman until she delivers" (AD 2157, Hakim); a man married and found her pregnant — the Prophet ﷺ separated them (Bayhaqi; weak chain — Ibn Jurayj, mudallis). Shafi'is and Hanafis: allowed — prohibition concerns a woman pregnant from a valid marriage (protecting lineage); "the child belongs to the bed, and the fornicator gets stones" (B, M) — the zani's sperm has no sanctity.
- **Author's preferred view:** with Shafi'is/Hanafis, but with conditions: (1) only the man from whose act she is pregnant may marry her while pregnant; no one else until delivery; (2) both must repent first; (3) if the case reaches court and is proven, hadd is applied (after delivery) and marriage considered afterwards.
- **Further arguments (p.444– ):** (1) no evidence prohibits it — the prohibition reports concern women in iddah from a valid marriage (Al-Baqarah 2:235: no nikah until the term ends); a woman pregnant from zina had no nikah/talaq so no iddah per the majority (Ibn Qudamah).
  Also Abu Bakr and Umar: no iddah for a woman who committed zina; this is the view of Thawri, Shafi'i and Hanafis; San'ani: the majority say no iddah is required. Baghawi: zani's sperm has no sanctity, so she may be married without iddah. Bayhaqi has a chapter: "No iddah for the zaniyah; whoever marries a woman pregnant from zina, his nikah is not annulled."
  (2) The reason for forbidding intercourse with a pregnant woman is "watering another's crop": "It isn't lawful for a believer to water another's crop with his water" (AD 2158 — about captive pregnant women). This doesn't apply when the zani marries the woman pregnant by him — it's his own seed.
  (3) A man may take back a wife he divorced while she's pregnant (his own child) — similarly the zani with the woman pregnant by him.
  (4) Delaying marriage until delivery increases disgrace for her family; marrying the zani mitigates it — though the child is not attributed to the zani ("the child belongs to the bed").
- **Marrying a zaniyah/zani — conditional (p.447–449):** chastity is required in both spouses: An-Nisa 4:24 ("seeking with your wealth, chaste, not fornicating"), Al-Ma'idah 5:5. A known zani/zaniyah may not be married until sincere repentance. **Marthad al-Ghanawi** wanted to marry 'Anaq, a prostitute in Makkah; An-Nur 24:3 was revealed — "don't marry her" (AD 2051, T 3177, N). Abdullah b. Amr: a Muslim asked to marry Umm Mahzul, a prostitute — forbidden by the same verse (Ah). Ahmad (via Ibn Kathir): a chaste man's nikah with a zaniyah is invalid until she repents, and vice versa. If she's pregnant from zina, only the man responsible may marry her while pregnant; others must wait until delivery (some fuqaha — Abu Hanifah, Muhammad, Shafi'i — allow nikah but no intercourse until delivery — Baghawi).

# Chapter 8 — Zihar, Ila', and Li'an (p.451–462)
Topics: zihar (calling wife mother/sister); ila' (oath to abstain); declaring one's wife haram for oneself; li'an.

## Section 1: Zihar (p.453–454)
- Zihar = husband saying to his wife "you are to me like my mother('s back)" or comparing her to any permanently-forbidden woman (sister, daughter, aunt, grandmother). It's a sin but doesn't end the nikah; **expiation (kaffarah)** is required before resuming intimacy (Al-Mujadilah 58:3–4):
  1. Nikah doesn't break.
  2. Kaffarah is required before resuming intimacy.
  3. Intimacy before kaffarah is a sin.
  4. Kaffarah: free a believing slave; else **fast 2 consecutive months**; else **feed 60 poor people**.
- Notes: kaffarah is on the man, not the woman; if a consecutive fast is broken without valid excuse, restart; if said without intending zihar (e.g. habitually calling wife "sister/mother") no kaffarah — but stop the habit; done in ignorance → forgiven.

## Declaring one's wife haram ("you are haram for me") (p.454–456)
- Scholars differ greatly (Ibn al-Qayyim lists 13 views in *Zad al-Ma'ad*; Qurtubi 18; Ibn Hajar more): some say talaq, some zihar, some an oath, some per intention, some meaningless.
- **Author's view:** unless he intended talaq, treat it as an **oath (yamin)** requiring the oath kaffarah — view of Abu Bakr, Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, Aisha, Ibn Abbas etc. Evidence: Anas — the Prophet ﷺ declared his slave-woman (Mariyah) haram for himself; At-Tahrim 66:1–2 revealed ("why do you forbid what Allah made lawful… Allah has ordained the dissolution of your oaths") (N 3411; Albani sahih). Ibn Abbas: "if a man declares his wife haram, it is an oath he expiates; you have in the Messenger of Allah a good example" (M 1473). Sa'id b. Jubayr: Ibn Abbas told a man "you lied, she isn't haram for you", recited 66:1, and said the heaviest expiation applies — freeing a slave (N 3449).

## Section 2: Ila' (oath to abstain from wife) (p.457–459)
- An oath not to have intercourse with one's wife (as discipline). Allowed for up to 4 months (Al-Baqarah 2:226). The Prophet ﷺ swore ila' for one month from his wives (B 5202, T 1201, IM 2072). For more than 4 months, or to harm her without reason, it is haram ("no harm and no reciprocal harm", IM 2340, Ah).
  1. If he returns before 4 months by breaking the oath → oath kaffarah ("if you swear and see better, expiate and do the better", B 6622, M 1652). Oath kaffarah (Al-Ma'idah 5:89): feed 10 poor, or clothe them, or free a slave; if unable, fast 3 days.
  2. If he swears for a fixed period ≤ 4 months and completes it — no kaffarah.
  3. If 4 months pass and he neither returns nor divorces, she may go to court, which compels him to return or divorce (2:226–227); Ibn Umar: after 4 months he must either retain kindly or divorce (B 5290); also from Uthman, Ali, Abu al-Darda', Aisha and 12 Companions (B).
  4. Talaq does **not** occur automatically when 4 months pass (Ibn Umar — B 5291).
  5. If he refuses both despite court, the court may annul the nikah.
  6. If he divorces, she observes the normal talaq iddah.

## Section 3: Li'an (p.460–462)
- Husband accuses wife of zina without four witnesses and she denies → the court conducts li'an (An-Nur 24:6–9): he swears four times by Allah he's truthful, fifth time invoking Allah's curse on himself if lying; she, to avert punishment, swears four times he's lying, fifth time invoking Allah's wrath on herself if he's truthful.
- **Rulings:** (1) after li'an, the man is spared the *qadhf* (slander) punishment and the woman the zina punishment (B 4747, M 1493); (2) only before a Shar'i court; (3) the judge first urges confession; if one confesses, punishment applies and no li'an (M 1493); (4) if the woman confesses → stoning; (5) if the man admits lying → 80 lashes for slander; (6) li'an only if neither confesses; (7) the couple are **separated forever** (B 5313, M 1493, AD 2250); (8) a child born after li'an is attributed to the mother (M 1494, AD 2259, T 1203); (9) the father and that child don't inherit from each other; the child and mother inherit from each other (Ah); (10) until li'an is complete, the child belongs to the father; if the man later admits lying, the child is attributed to him again (B 6750, M 1458); (11) no maintenance/housing for the woman after li'an (Ah, AD 2256, Bayhaqi); (12) the mahr already given isn't returned (B 5312, M 1493, AD 2257).

# Chapter 9 — Special Issues for Spouses (p.463–500)
Question list (p.463): does kissing/embracing break wudu or fast? looking at each other's bodies; demanding talaq from an impotent husband; mutual will to die together; demanding a separate house; repeating wudu after washing children's filth; using menses-delaying pills; removing facial/body hair; thinning eyebrows; long nails/nail polish; women cutting hair; reading Qur'an while impure; from whom to observe purdah and from whom not; high heels and tight clothes; sucking wife's breast; living with a husband who doesn't pray; nikah during menses; intercourse during pregnancy; prayer of a woman with istihadah; artificial hair; women's hair joining (paranda); limits of Shar'i hijab and face covering; family drivers & non-mahram women; women driving; women working alongside men.

## Section 1: Special issues for husband and wife (p.465–472) — mostly fatwas of Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymin, Ibn Jibrin, Saudi Permanent Committee
- **Kissing/touching doesn't break wudu:** Aisha — the Prophet ﷺ kissed (a wife) and didn't renew wudu (AD 177, N 170); kissed a wife then went to pray (AD 178); her feet were in his sajdah place and he'd touch them (B 382, M). Ibn Abbas, Ali, Ubayy, Ata', Tawus, Hasan, Sa'id b. Jubayr, Abu Hanifah etc. agree. An-Nisa 4:43 "*lamastum al-nisa'*" = intercourse (Ibn Abbas).
- **Kissing/embracing doesn't break the fast** (unless intercourse): the Prophet ﷺ kissed and embraced while fasting but had most control over his desire (B 1927, M 1106). **Intercourse during a Ramadan fast:** make-up plus kaffarah — free a slave; else fast 2 consecutive months; else feed 60 poor (B 1936, M 1111). If unable at all, it lapses — the man given dates to give in charity was told to feed his own family (Al-Baqarah 2:286).
- **Sucking the wife's breast:** Shaykh Muhammad b. Ibrahim: no harm if no milk is swallowed; if milk is swallowed it's improper but doesn't make her haram, as fosterage only counts in the first two years (T). Ibn Uthaymin: spouses may enjoy each other in any way except what Allah forbade (menses, anal, during ihram/i'tikaf, before zihar kaffarah).
- **Seeing each other's whole body:** allowed (Al-Mu'minun 23:5–7) — Ibn Uthaymin.
- **Husband who doesn't pray (p.469–470):** a woman whose husband abandons prayer and Ramadan fasting and is suspicious/obstructive — Ibn Uthaymin: one who abandons prayer altogether is a kafir and the nikah ends (Al-Mumtahanah 60:10); she shouldn't stay with him unless he repents.
- **Husband infertile — may she seek divorce? (p.470–471):** yes; she has a right to children; if he won't divorce, a qadi can annul — Ibn Uthaymin (view of the people of knowledge).
- **Washing each other's body after death (p.471):** allowed — Asma bint Umays washed Abu Bakr; Fatimah willed that Ali wash her (Ibn Baz). He may see her, touch her, and lower her into the grave.
- **Asking for a separate home from the husband (p.471–472):** a wife whose brother-in-law lives with them and she must observe hijab — Ibn Jibrin: if full hijab and no seclusion can be maintained, separate living is better; if risk/discomfort exists, she may request a separate house.

## Section 2: Special issues for women (p.473– )
- **Infant's vomit on clothes:** for a suckling infant, sprinkle water on the spot and pray (Ibn Baz).
- **Infant's urine:** a nursing baby boy's urine — sprinkling water suffices (the Prophet ﷺ sprinkled water on a boy's urine); a girl's urine must be washed (Ibn Uthaymin).
- **Washing children's private parts after wudu:** Saudi Committee: if you touch the child's private part directly, wudu breaks (as with touching one's own); Ibn Uthaymin: touching without desire doesn't break wudu — just wash hands.
- **Ghusl from janabah vs menses; undoing hair:** no difference between men's and women's ghusl; braids needn't be undone for janabah — three handfuls on the head suffice; Umm Salamah asked; "it's enough to pour three handfuls" (M).
- **Mustahadah prayer (irregular bleeding):** a 52-year-old with irregular bleeding — Ibn Baz: bleeding after ~50 when periods have become irregular is *istihadah* (dam fasad), not menses; she prays, fasts, may have intercourse; makes wudu for each prayer (Fatimah bint Abi Hubaysh — "make wudu for each prayer", B).
- **Brownish discharge before period:** Ibn Jibrin: if before the known days, it's dam fasad — she should pray; at known time, it's menses. **Yellow/brown after purity:** Ibn Uthaymin — not menses; after a clear sign of purity (white discharge), pray and fast.
- **Menses-delaying pills (p.477):** permitted for Hajj or Ramadan if no harm — consult a doctor (Saudi Committee; Ibn Uthaymin: allowed but better avoided for health).
- **Miscarriage (p.477–479):** if the foetus had human form (head, hands, feet visible), the bleeding is *nifas* — no prayer/fast until purity or 40 days max; may become pure earlier (ghusl, pray). If no human form yet, the bleeding is *istihadah* — she prays, wudu for each prayer, may combine Zuhr/Asr and Maghrib/Isha (Hamnah bint Jahsh) (Ibn Baz).
- **Nikah during menses (p.479–481):** valid — the prohibition during menses is on talaq and intercourse, not on nikah. Talaq during menses or a tuhr with intercourse is forbidden (At-Talaq 65:1; Ibn Umar case). Talaq during pregnancy is valid (contrary to popular belief). Ibn Uthaymin: if nikah occurs during menses, better that he not go to her until purity.
- **Intercourse with pregnant wife (p.481–482):** allowed (Al-Baqarah 2:223; Al-Mu'minun 23:5–6) — nothing forbids it; only menses/nifas and anal intercourse are forbidden (Ibn Uthaymin).
- **Women cutting hair (p.482):** Ibn Baz: shaving the head is forbidden for women; trimming length is fine, especially for ease/husband's liking, without imitating disbelievers or men (some scholars, e.g. Albani's student, also allow).
- **Artificial hair/wigs (p.483):** Ibn Baz: haram — like *wasl* (hair extensions) which the Prophet ﷺ cursed; even if husband wants it. Exception: to cover a defect (e.g. a woman with no hair) it's allowed — like Arfajah's gold nose (medical/cosmetic correction allowed; beautification beyond creation forbidden).
- **Removing unusual facial hair (p.484):** plucking eyebrows (namṣ) is haram and cursed; shaving them is disputed — avoid. Unusual hair (e.g. moustache/beard hair on a woman, hair on cheeks) may be removed (Ibn Uthaymin). **Thinning eyebrows:** not allowed (Ibn Baz).
- **Removing body hair (p.485–486):** armpit/pubic hair removal is Sunnah (plucking armpits, shaving pubic hair best, any method OK); women removing leg/arm hair — no harm; removing eyebrow hair even at husband's request — not allowed (namṣ is cursed) (Ibn Baz).
- **Long nails and nail polish (p.485):** growing nails is against the Sunnah — five fitrah acts include trimming nails (M); the Prophet ﷺ set a 40-day limit (M); also resembles animals/disbelievers. Nail polish must be removed for wudu, as it blocks water (Ibn Baz).
- **Paranda (hair-tie/braid extensions of thread):** permitted — Hafiz Thanaullah Madani (Abu Dawud: Sa'id b. Jubayr allowed it; Layth's fatwa).
- **Menstruating woman reading Qur'an / du'a books (p.486–488):** Ibn Baz: allowed to read du'a books containing verses, and even to recite Qur'an from memory without touching the mushaf (no sound proof to forbid; "a menstruating/junub person shouldn't read Qur'an" is weak — Isma'il b. Ayyash narrating from Hijazis). Junub (sexual impurity) person shouldn't recite (Ali's report) since he can bathe quickly; menses lasts long, so she may recite to avoid forgetting. Author's note: the junub may recite without touching per some (Ibn Abbas?).
- **High heels:** at least makruh — deceptive height, risk of falling, medically harmful (Ibn Baz).
- **Nose ring:** permitted — it's customary adornment like earrings (Ibn Baz). (Abu Muhammad's *Fatawa al-Mar'ah* also cited.)

## Section 3: Covering and hijab (p.489–497)
- **Shar'i hijab:** covering all of the body that must be covered; the **face** is the main place of attraction, so covering it is most important. Ibn Baz: claiming hijab covers only head, neck, chest, legs, arms but not face/hands is strange; the face is the essence of beauty (illustration: men choose brides by their faces).
- **Why face covering is obligatory:** Al-Ahzab 33:53 (ask the Prophet's ﷺ wives from behind a screen — purer for hearts — general ruling); Al-Ahzab 33:59 (draw down jilbabs); An-Nur 24:31 (don't display adornment except to listed relatives). Applies at home and abroad, Muslim or non-Muslim land (Ibn Baz).
- **Family drivers and non-mahram women (p.491–492):** "No man is alone with a woman but Shaytan is the third" (T). Driver alone with a woman — forbidden khalwah (Ibn Jibrin). Travelling alone with a non-mahram driver within the city — not allowed; with two or more women or men present (no seclusion) — allowed (Ibn Baz). Travel requires a mahram ("No woman travels except with a mahram", B, M) — by land, sea or air.
- **Women driving (p.492):** Saudi fatwa: impermissible due to resulting evils — mixing, unveiling, etc.; means to haram are haram.
- **Women working alongside men (p.492–493):** mixing in the workplace leads to khalwah/fitnah; contradicts verses telling women to stay at home; strong evidence forbids mixing (Saudi fatwa).
- **From whom to observe purdah (p.493–497)** — Maulana Abdul Rahman Kilani, *Ahkam-e-Satr wa Hijab*: five categories:
  1. **Strangers (ajanib):** non-relatives — full hijab including face and hands, unless necessary.
  2. **Mahrams:** those she can never marry (except husband): husband, father, father-in-law, sons, stepsons, brothers, nephews (brothers'/sisters' sons), paternal & maternal uncles — and their extensions (grandfathers on both sides; grandsons; full/half brothers; nephews' descendants). Also all equivalent foster relations: Aflah, Abu al-Qu'ays's brother (Aisha's foster uncle) — "let him in, he is your uncle" (B, M). In doubtful cases observe hijab: Sawdah told to veil from the boy (Zam'ah's slave-girl's son) resembling Utbah — "the child belongs to the bed… veil from him, Sawdah" (B).
  3. **Husband's relatives:** brother-in-law, cousins etc. — "Beware of entering upon women." "What about the in-law (*hamw*)?" "The in-law is death" (B, M). Most negligence happens here.
  4. **Wife's relatives (sister-in-law and brother-in-law, *sali/behnoi*):** in Punjab, the sister-in-law is joked with as "half-wife" — gravely wrong. Since one cannot be married to two sisters at once (An-Nisa 4:23), she's only temporarily forbidden → hijab is required. Asma bint Abi Bakr (Aisha's sister) came in thin clothes; the Prophet ﷺ turned away: "when a woman reaches puberty, nothing should be seen of her but this and this" (face and hands) (AD) — the narration is mursal and weak (Khalid b. Durayk didn't meet Aisha) but shows face/hands not to be awrah in that view; thin clothes are haram.
  5. **Other relatives:** cousins etc. Allah exempted elderly women from strict hijab; the criterion is the age gap and sexual inclination. Children/elderly men: no strict hijab, though preferable; if desire exists — hijab required (Kilani p.82–86).

# Chapter 10 — Polygamy (ta'addud al-azwaj) (p.499–548)
Topics: permissibility and wisdom (in light of modern needs); Pakistani society, second marriage and practical wisdom; second marriage is Sunnah, not obligatory.

## Section 1: Permissibility and wisdom (p.501– ) — adapted from Prof. Saeed Mujtaba Saeedi's article (*Muhaddith* monthly)
- Modern contradiction: the West spreads obscenity (nude pictures, films, mixing) while promoting monogamy; Encyclopaedia Britannica: industrialization, mass education and Western prestige caused polygamy to wane; monogamy also prescribed by Roman Catholics and Hindus.
- Some modern Muslims call polygamy unjust or regressive and reinterpret verses (e.g. a "leading hadith-denier" claiming the Qur'an's principle is one wife at a time and An-Nisa's permission was only for war-time orphans/widows).
- **History:** polygamy existed in prior Shari'ahs:
  - **Lamech** (descendant of Adam/Nuh) had two wives, Adah and Zillah (Bible, Genesis 4).
  - **Ibrahim:** Hajar and Sarah at the same time, and later Qanturah bint Yaqtan (Ibn Kathir; Genesis 16, 25).
  - **Ya'qub:** married sisters Leah and Rachel and their two handmaids (Genesis) — also shows combining sisters was allowed in his law.
  - **Esau, son of Ishaq,** married several wives incl. Isma'il's daughter (Genesis 26, 28).
  - **Dawud:** 99 wives (Khazin's tafsir; Bible 2 Samuel 3 lists some).
  - **Sulayman:** "Sulayman said: tonight I will go round 90 (or 99/100) women…" (B); Bible (1 Kings 11) gives 700 wives and 300 concubines.
  - **Musa:** married Shu'ayb's daughter Zipporah and a Cushite woman (Exodus 2; Numbers 12) — some say four wives.
  - **Hindus:** kings and Krishna had many wives; Raja Dasharath (Rama's father) had three; Krishna many; Draupadi had five husbands (Pandavas) — polyandry in Mahabharata.
- Pre-Islamic Arabs had unlimited wives, and sometimes a woman had several men (*nikah al-raht*). Islam set a balanced limit of **four** (Qurtubi: Allah confined pre-Islamic excess to four).

### Polygamy in Shari'ah (p.507–510)
- An-Nisa 4:3: "if you fear you won't be just to orphans, marry women you like — two, three, four; if you fear you won't be just, then one…". Aisha's explanation: guardians wanting to marry orphan girls in their care without fair mahr were told to marry other women instead (B).
- Rebuttal of **Ghulam Ahmad Parwez** (claimed polygamy is conditional only on war/orphan situations): the condition about orphans relates to treating orphans unjustly, not to permitting polygamy only in emergencies. An-Nisa 4:129 ("you will never be able to be perfectly just between wives… so don't incline completely to one leaving the other suspended") refers to emotional inclination; the required justice is in outward treatment (Ibn Kathir — the hadith of the man with two wives coming paralysed on one side). Maududi and Amin Ahsan Islahi: conditional phrasing doesn't restrict permission (like An-Nur 24:33 "if they desire chastity" doesn't mean forcing slaves into prostitution is allowed otherwise).

### Hadith (p.510–512)
- **Ghaylan b. Salamah al-Thaqafi** had 10 wives when he became Muslim — "choose four and separate from the rest" (T, IM, Ah, Muwatta; Suyuti; sahih per many chains).
- **Qays b. al-Harith** had 8 wives — "choose four" (AD, IM).
- **Nawfal b. Mu'awiyah** had 5 — "separate one, keep four" (Shafi'i).
- Conclusions: polygamy hadiths are numerous; muhaddithun gave chapters; max four at a time.

### Rightly-guided Caliphs and polygamy (p.512–513)
- **Abu Bakr:** married more than one; at death, Habibah bint Kharijah was pregnant (gave birth to Umm Kulthum), and Asma bint Umays washed him per his will.
- **Umar:** had more than one wife at death — Atikah bint Zayd and Umm Kulthum bint Ali.
- **Ali:** married several after Fatimah; his son Hasan was famous for many marriages (Ali told Kufans not to marry their daughters to Hasan, as he divorces much).

### Consensus (p.513–515)
- Ijma' that a man may marry up to four; Sarakhsi, Qurtubi, Ibn Hajar, Khazin, Ibn Qudamah. Some Rafidis/Zahiri dissenters claimed up to nine (2+3+4) or eighteen — rejected as against ijma' and Sunnah.

### Wisdom of polygamy (p.515–523)
1. **Male nature:** Ibn al-Qayyim refutes the claim that women's desire exceeds men's; men's nature ("hotter temperament") needs more (*I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*).
2. **Male desire for variety:** Muhammad Hanif Nadwi — monogamy failed in Europe; Shah Wali Allah — men's nature tends to multiple wives, Shari'ah limited it to four.
3. **External stimuli:** men face constant arousal outside home (Maududi on Western obscenity, mixing, films); one wife may not suffice.
4. **Protecting chastity:** wife's menses, pregnancy, nifas, illness — man may be driven to sin; Ibn al-Qayyim and Shah Wali Allah: for some, one wife isn't enough for chastity. The West, refusing polygamy legally, has mistresses and affairs (Maududi: Western men accept illicit relationships but reject a second marriage as a crime).
5. **Male superiority (qiwamah)** — men's greater responsibilities (Ibn al-Qayyim, *Bada'i' al-Fawa'id*, *I'lam*); author's footnote: superiority is in roles; as human beings men and women have equal rights.
6. **Increasing the Ummah's numbers** — "marry the loving, fertile…"; Shah Wali Allah: the greatest aim of marriage is offspring; a man can father children with several women at once.
7. **Social necessity:** after wars, many widows/orphans; Amin Ahsan Islahi; Nadwi: post-war Europe needed polygamy; Indian Muslims as minority could grow by it (Nadwi).
8. **Western society's failure:** illegitimate children, broken families, social burden; the West rejects polygamy but tolerates promiscuity.

### Why only four? (p.523–524)
- Before Islam no limit; four allows justice. Ibn al-Qayyim (*Ighathat al-Lahfan*): limit prevents injustice while still sufficing against zina; Shah Wali Allah: beyond 3 nights' absence from a wife is hardship; Ibn al-Qayyim: returning to a wife after 3 nights is reasonable. **Ashraf Ali Thanawi's** explanation: pregnancy recognized after ~3 months → with four wives, each cycle of 3 months covers the next; by the time of the 4th, the first has delivered — so four is the natural limit. Slaves/concubines had no limit, but Islam encouraged freeing slaves, and slavery no longer exists.

### Why only one husband for a woman? (p.525–527)
- **Lineage confusion:** a story attributed to Abu Hanifah's daughter (women asked why men may have four but women not; she had each pour milk into one bowl and asked each to take back her own — impossible; likewise, paternity would be lost). Author notes the story is doubtful (Shibli: Abu Hanifah had no daughter named Hanifah) but the wisdom stands. Shah Wali Allah and Ibn al-Qayyim: allowing multiple husbands would destroy lineage.
- **Women's physical/biological constraints:** menses, pregnancy, nifas; a woman can carry only one man's child at a time (Nadwi).
- **Strife:** male jealousy would lead to killing and chaos (Ibn al-Qayyim).
- **Conclusion:** the West condemns legal polygamy but practises illegal "polygamy" (mistresses, illegitimate children, collapsing families); Islam's permission protects chastity and provides for widows/orphans. Polygamy is feared by the disbelieving world because it increases Muslim numbers.
- References list for the chapter (p.529–531).

## Section 2: Pakistani society, second marriage and practical wisdom (p.532–548)
- **Second marriage is Sunnah, not obligatory (fard).** Some are extreme against it; others promote it as though it were nearly obligatory. It is permitted/recommended **only when conditions are met** — otherwise it can bring sin.

### Conditions of polygamy (p.533–539)
1. **Sufficient financial means:** marriage is responsibility — housing, food, clothing, children's care/education. Each wife has a right to a separate dwelling (the Prophet ﷺ gave each wife her own room). Ibn Qudamah: two wives can't be placed in one dwelling without their consent. In Pakistan, an average salaried man (e.g. Rs 4–5,000/month) can't afford two households — example of a religious-movement worker whose second marriage became a burden on the movement and ruined two homes.
2. **Physical ability to fulfil conjugal rights:** e.g. a 60-year-old marrying an 18-year-old will likely fail her rights; though if she freely consents, it's not forbidden. Men often seek second marriages in old age when the first wife has aged — this often leads to injustice; better for capable men to do so in youth.
3. **Not merely for lust:** An-Nisa 4:24 — "seeking chastity, not lust"; marriage is responsibility; fasting is prescribed for those who can't bear it.
4. **Justice between wives:** equality in maintenance, housing, time, nights, gifts; inner affection is excused. An-Nisa 4:3 — if you fear injustice, one only. "Whoever has two wives and inclines to one comes on the Day of Resurrection with half his body fallen" (T, Darimi). Common Pakistani pattern: second (younger) wife is favoured; first wife and children are neglected — even forced to do menial work — sometimes divorced. Author's anecdote: a respected religious worker abandoned his first wife and 5–6 children for a young second wife; when asked why, he said "she was worse than animals to me" — author: "yet you had 5–6 children with her!"

### Excuses for injustice (p.540–541)
- The Sawdah story — she gave her turn to Aisha fearing divorce in old age — is used to justify discarding older wives; author: the report that the Prophet ﷺ intended to divorce her lacks a sound chain (Fath al-Bari); Sawdah herself chose to give her day (AD, sahih). An-Nisa 4:129 concerns inner love only; outward rights (maintenance, clothing, housing, turns) must be equal.

### Misusing the Prophet's ﷺ Sunnah (p.542)
- Some argue: the Prophet ﷺ married many despite poverty, so marriage brings barakah and helps da'wah. Author: his situation was unique (special divine support; his marriages strengthened da'wah and tribal alliances). In Pakistan, second marriages usually harm religious work and create family rivalry.

### Wrong analogy with Arabs (p.543–544)
- In Arab societies polygamy is common and accepted; but there, women have independent wealth (big inheritances, own homes/businesses), and separate homes. In Pakistan, women depend economically on husbands, families live together, inheritance is denied — a second wife often causes crisis, divorce, and destitution. Maulana Abdul Rahman Kilani (*Taysir al-Qur'an*): in Pakistan where a woman's independent ownership isn't recognized, polygamy creates many problems; ~95% of men don't need it; where a man needs it, the permission exists — but Arab practice can't simply be copied.

### Blameworthy practice among some Arabs and Afghans (p.545)
- Some keep four wives and, wanting a new one, divorce one of the four simply to stay within the limit — lust-driven and blameworthy. Some Afghans reportedly declare one wife "khalifah" (cutting off conjugal relations without divorce) to marry a fifth — worse than the Arab practice. (Author notes not all Arabs/Afghans do this.)

### Marrying widows — a welcome trend (p.545–546)
- Hindu-influenced Muslims looked down on widow remarriage; Sayyid Ahmad Shahid's movement revived it. Author praises **Jama'at al-Da'wah** (amir Hafiz Muhammad Saeed) for encouraging marriage with widows of martyrs, but urges: give priority to men who want a second marriage to widows, not to virgins, so the hundreds of martyrs' widows aren't neglected.

# Chapter 11 — Islam and Birth Control (p.547–577)
Topics: Shar'i status of family planning and national population planning; permissible cases and limits.

## Section 1: Islam and family planning (p.549– ) — largely from Maulana **Sayyid Abul A'la Maududi**'s *Islam aur Zabt-e-Wiladat* (appendix essay)
- **Nature of the issue:** "family planning" is an old idea under a new name — fear that population grows faster than resources. Malthus: population grows geometrically (1, 2, 4, 8…) while food grows arithmetically (1, 2, 3, 4…). Old methods: infanticide, abortion, contraception; modern: drugs/devices, sterilisation — called birth control, family planning, etc.
- **Is the fear correct? (p.551–556):** history disproves it — population never grew as Malthus predicted. Resources aren't fixed: humans keep discovering new ones (coal, steam, oil, electricity, atom). Population is also a *producer*, not only a consumer; need drives innovation.
  - Examples: **Germany** — 1880 population 45m; people emigrated due to hunger; by 1914, 68m and needing foreign workers; post-WWII West Germany absorbed ~10m refugees yet prospered. **Holland** — ~1m in 18th century, 10m by 1950, on reclaimed land, exporting food. **Britain** — 12m (1789) → 46m (1913) despite emigration, and wealthier. Worldwide: population surged since the 18th century, yet living standards rose dramatically.
- **Correct remedy:** increase resources, not reduce population.
- **Who is the real planner? (p.557–560):** biological description — hundreds of millions of sperm per ejaculation, ~400,000 immature eggs, about one egg per cycle, hundreds of opportunities over a lifetime; only the Creator decides which unions result in conception, sex, abilities, appearance. Human population, despite enormous reproductive capacity, has grown only to ~3 billion (then) over thousands of years — evidence that a divine plan controls it.
- **Why "family" planning instead of "population" planning? (p.561–562):** true population planning would require state control of reproduction (abolishing marriage/family; state-run breeding) — unacceptable; so it is pushed onto individual families via appeals to self-interest (higher living standard, children's welfare) and mass distribution of contraceptives.
- **Consequences (p.562– ):**
  1. **Population decline:** individuals prioritise comfort over national need; e.g. **France** — first to adopt widespread birth control in the 19th century; deaths began exceeding births (1890–1911 and onwards; by 1933 most departments had more deaths than births); weakened France faced defeat in the world wars. Warning for Pakistan surrounded by large populations.
  2. **Moral destruction:** the "self-interest" mindset makes people see children, elderly parents, orphaned relatives, the sick as burdens.
  3. **Spread of zina:** easy contraception removes the last barrier (fear of pregnancy), especially amid co-education, obscene media, and legal obstacles to early marriage and polygamy — leading to widespread illicit relations.
- **Individual vs. mass movement (p.566):** a married couple, for a genuine personal need, using contraception on a scholar's fatwa or doctor's advice is a limited matter; a mass campaign spreading contraceptives to everyone is entirely different and produces the harms above.
- **Islamic view (p.567–570):** hadiths on 'azl dealt with individual questions, not a mass movement; using them to justify a national birth-control campaign is a false analogy. The movement's premise (provision will shrink) contradicts the Qur'an: provision is Allah's responsibility — Al-Ankabut 29:60, Hud 11:6, Adh-Dhariyat 51:58, Ash-Shura 42:12, Al-Hijr 15:20–21, Al-Ankabut 29:17 (seek provision from Allah). Al-An'am 6:151, Al-Isra 17:31 ("don't kill your children for fear of poverty — We provide for them and you"): the error was both killing *and* the belief that children cause poverty; preventing children from that fear repeats the second error. (End of Maududi's piece.)

## Section 2: Birth control — permitted cases and limits (p.571–575)
- In principle, birth control/family planning from fear of scarcity — as state or individual policy — is not allowed; but for a genuine reason (e.g. medical), individual use is permitted.
- **Methods described:** (1) condom / loop (IUD); (2) contraceptive drugs/injections; (3) sterilisation (permanent — e.g. tying/removing parts; removing the womb); (4) abortion.
- **Medical necessity permits it:** if pregnancy would definitely harm a woman's health/life (e.g. after repeated caesareans doctors advise against pregnancy), she may use suitable contraception while the need lasts.
- **Abortion for medical necessity (p.572–573):** abortion is a sin, but if the mother's life is truly at risk from pregnancy/delivery, abortion is allowed: the mother is the "root" (asl) and the child the "branch"; saving the certain life takes precedence over the potential life; necessity makes the forbidden permissible. If both can be saved, doctors must try. Decision based on expert doctors' advice.
- **Analogy with 'azl:** 'azl (withdrawal) was practised by Companions without necessity; the Prophet ﷺ didn't forbid it (Jabir, M 1440) though he called it "the hidden burial" (M; AD 3882, T 2076) — so 'azl is disliked but not haram; modern temporary methods (condom, loop) for spacing etc. may be judged by analogy — but cannot justify a collective movement. Saudi Permanent Committee holds the same balanced view.
- **Contraceptive pills (Saudi Committee fatwa, p.574):** using them out of fear of many children or expenses — not allowed; if pregnancy harms the woman, or she can't deliver normally and needs surgery each time, it's allowed per a trustworthy doctor; if pills harm her, avoid them.
- **Family planning (Ibn Baz, p.574–575):** the Council of Senior Scholars ruled contraceptive pills impermissible as a general policy because Allah legislated means to increase offspring; "marry the loving, fertile… I will boast of your numbers" (AD, N); "...before the prophets on the Day of Resurrection" (Ah). The Ummah needs numbers for worship, jihad, and protection. Allowed only for necessity: e.g. womb illness/disease harming the woman, too many children close together harming her health — then temporary use (e.g. 2 years during breastfeeding) to recover and raise children is fine. Not allowed for career/comfort/keeping a job or standard of living.

# Chapter 12 (Appendix) — Easy Household Tips and Tested Remedies (p.577–603)
Compiled by **Mrs. Mubashir Hussain Lahori**. Three parts: kitchen & food tips; beauty tips; general home remedies.

## Section 1: Kitchen and food tips (p.578– )
- **Too much salt in curry:** add a ball of kneaded dough (or a raw potato) for a while, then remove; or add some gram flour mixed in water / lemon juice.
- **Too much chilli:** add whole garam masala (cinnamon, cardamom, white cumin, cloves) fried in a little ghee; or some yoghurt; or a bit of fried/roasted gram flour.
- **Too much turmeric:** put a piece of white cloth in the curry briefly to absorb it.
- **Fish smell:** wash with water containing borax (suhaga) or rub with lemon juice/salt, or gram flour paste for 15 minutes, then wash.
- **Cauliflower smell:** add a few drops of lemon juice to the cooking water.
- **Trotters (paye) smell:** add white cumin, cinnamon, cloves while cooking. **Cleaning trotters:** soak in boiled water 3–4 minutes, then scrape.
- **Bitter moongray (radish pods):** peel, salt for an hour, wash.
- **Burnt meat curry:** transfer meat to a new pot, add a little milk and re-fry.
- **Bitterness of new turnips/karela:** peel, salt, rest, wash (sugar/jaggery is optional).
- **Peeling garlic:** soak in hot water 10 minutes.
- **Crisp pakoras:** add a little garlic paste to the batter.
- **Softening peas:** add mint while boiling.
- **Tenderising chickpeas/meat:** add 2–4 potatoes while cooking; if lentils/chickpeas still won't soften, add about half a cup of milk and check after 10–15 minutes.
- **New rice not breaking:** wash 2–3 times, soak in warm water before cooking.
- **Making cream from milk:** mix about 1½ tsp gelatine into milk, beat it well and chill it, then skim off the cream. Another method: boil the milk, then cool it quickly in an ice-water bath and lift the cream.
- **Prevent cream spoiling:** add a pinch of baking soda.
- **Dough not fermenting/sticking:** apply a little ghee; keep covered with a damp cloth; avoid too much water.
- **Using split milk:** strain to make paneer (cottage cheese); use with salt, chilli, onion, or in curry.
- **Freshening frozen meat:** soak in half a cup of milk for 10 minutes before cooking.
- **Mending cracked china:** boil in milk on low flame for ~45 minutes; milk proteins fill fine cracks.
- **Cleaning plastic utensils:** wash with hot water + detergent/soda using a nylon brush.
- **Peeling boiled eggs:** add salt to the water; plunge in cold water after boiling.
- **Burnt pots:** boil water with 2 spoons of salt in it; scrub after.
- **Glass not cracking from hot liquids:** place a metal spoon in the glass before pouring.
- **Milk not burning in steel pans:** rinse with water first.
- **Frying eggs without sticking:** sprinkle a little salt in the pan before adding oil; don't use eggs straight from the fridge in very hot oil.
- **Ice trays sticking in freezer:** place a plastic mat under them.
- **Apples not browning:** dip slices in cold salted water.
- **Rice weevils:** mix rice with whole dried red chillies/salt; about 1 kg salt per maund.
- **Burnt hand:** immediately apply wet salt (or cold water) — prevents blisters.
- **Thawing frozen meat quickly:** soak in salted water.
- **Preserving fish:** freeze in water; or gut, salt, and store in a cold place; rinse with water/gram flour before use.
- **Pure red chilli:** grind at home (market chilli is often adulterated).
- **Salt clumping:** add a few cloves and some rice grains. **Smelly jars/bottles:** soak with water and a little baking soda (or hot water + washing powder), shake, rinse, keep lids off. **Fridge stains:** rub with toothpaste, then wipe. **Flies in kitchen:** keep mint (in water) there. **Peeling tomatoes:** hold over a flame on a fork until the skin cracks.
- **Freezer odours:** keep a little baking soda in the bottom shelf. **Testing eggs:** a fresh egg sinks in water; a bad one floats. **Onion smell on hands:** rub with salt and water. **Fish bone in throat:** drink lemon juice or swallow a lump of dough/banana. **Fine garlic paste:** add a pinch of salt when grinding. **Spluttering ghee:** sprinkle a pinch of flour.

## Section 2: Beauty tips (p.587–590)
- **Acne/blemishes:** lemon-peel paste with glycerine as a mask for 15 minutes; rub lemon peel on lips to remove dark colour. Massage face with the inside of an orange/kinnow peel ~20 minutes daily; or wash face several times a day with warm salt water (3 tsp salt per cup) for 10–15 days.
- **Glowing face (winter):** almond oil + honey mix on face/hands at night for half an hour, then wash with warm water.
- **Freckles:** a paste of ground seeds in milk applied at night and washed off in the morning (see p.588 for the exact recipe).
- **Long, thick hair:** ber (jujube) leaves paste for 10 minutes before shampoo; use mild shampoo; soak mustard seeds overnight and wash hair with that water for shine.
- **Tea rinse:** boiled tea water as conditioner after washing.
- **Darkening hair:** soak dry amla in an iron pot overnight; wash hair with the water.
- **Healthy hair:** grate lauki (bottle gourd), cook in mustard oil until brown, strain and store; apply as needed.
- **Soft skin:** lemon juice with olive oil massage at night; dried orange peel powder + raw milk paste for an hour before bath; turmeric + cream/milk paste before bathing.
- **Facial spots:** 1 tsp lemon juice + 1 tsp salt, apply for 10 minutes.
- **Wrinkles:** apply buffalo-milk cream (malai) at night, wash with soap in the morning.
- **Reducing belly:** lie on your stomach with a pillow under the belly for 15 minutes each morning before getting up.
- **Weight loss:** warm water with honey (and lemon) on an empty stomach; lemon in salad/food; kalonji powder with sugar; walking/exercise; avoid sweets, rice, ghee; eat cucumber, radish; garlic cloves on an empty stomach; gram-flour roti; apples.

## Section 3: General home remedies (p.591–600)
- **Mosquitoes:** burn dried orange/kinnow peels for smoke. **Constipation:** a cup of milk with two spoons of honey at night. **Bleeding wounds:** spider webs (traditional) or press hard; tie a clean cloth. **Child's nosebleed/mouth bleed:** sugar on the spot. **Easier labour:** a small spoon of cumin boiled in water, reduced to half, with ghee — taken near delivery.
- **Shoe polish shine:** a few drops of kerosene. **Lizards:** egg shells hung near curtains/lights. **Stuck ice trays:** sprinkle salt. **Dark elbows:** rub with lemon halves + sugar/turmeric. **Migraine:** almond oil drops in the nose; smell a cut onion.
- **Salt + ghee + honey:** salt and desi ghee mixture for constipation; salt + honey for teething gums; salt and neem-leaf bath for itching/rashes.
- **Foot odour:** boil one round brinjal in water and soak feet daily for a week. **Strong teeth:** roasted alum + salt powder as tooth powder 2–3 times a week. **Mouth ulcers:** borax + glycerine (or honey). **Chapped lips:** fresh milk cream; warm ghee in the navel. **Diarrhoea:** 2–3 spoons of sugar in half a glass of water.
- **Cough:** black pepper with salt from the palm; ~3 times a day. **Bad breath:** chew dry coriander seeds. **Salt remedies:** salt water for eyes/throat gargles, cold water with salt for stomach ache, salt + oil massage for dry skin.
- **Children's health:** almonds, raisins and dried dates ground, a spoon with milk. **Increasing breast milk:** soaked white chickpeas (chanay) eaten with sugar in the morning.
- **Phlegm:** 11 raisins with black pepper daily. **Weakness/fatigue:** a glass of water with tea-spoon of sugar and a little salt, twice. **Itching:** rub onion juice. **Dirty mirror:** clean with spirit or wet newspaper. **Slippery floor:** bleach + washing powder scrub.
- **Shining gold jewellery:** soak in soapy water with a little turmeric 4–5 hours, brush. **Damp/musty home in rains:** burn loban (frankincense). **Darker henna:** after it dries, hold hands over clove smoke on a hot tawa. **Whiter laundry:** a little alum or chalk in hot wash water. **Cement on steel pots:** wrap in a wet cloth/paper and heat, then scrape.
- **Brighter white clothes:** a spoon of salt with blue (neel) in rinse water. **Paint smell:** cut onions in the room. **Chewing gum on clothes:** freeze the cloth then peel. **Dustbin odour:** lemon peels / vegetable peels inside. **Cloudy glass bottles:** shake with washing powder and crushed egg shells.
- **Homemade tooth powder:** ash of a burnt dried fuel-cake + alum + salt. **Stuck glasses:** cold water in the top glass, hot water around the bottom. **Tea stains:** salt paste then detergent after 10 minutes. **Grease/oil stains:** absorbent paper under, petrol/spirit on cotton from behind, then wash. **Cleaning glass:** salt-water wash, dry with newspaper.
- **Keeping vegetables fresh:** store separately in a cool, dry place; wrap leafy greens in a damp cloth. **Preserving tomatoes:** boil with salt, blend, bottle with a layer of oil. **Furniture polish:** spirit + lac/wax twice a year. **Skin diseases (itching, eczema, boils):** a homemade ointment of mustard oil and other simple ingredients — apply after warm wash for a week.
- **Pests:** fleas — garlic and sulphur smoke; bedbugs — boiled water on bed joints/cracks; ants — kerosene / turmeric; mosquitoes — kafoor/coal smoke; termites — soak/neem-leaves smoke; flies — black pepper + sugar + milk/cream bait.
