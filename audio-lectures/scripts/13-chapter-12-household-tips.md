# Lecture 13 — Chapter 12, Appendix: Easy Household Tips and Tested Remedies

Assalamu alaykum. This is Lecture 13, on the final chapter, pages 577 to 603. It is an appendix compiled by the author's wife, Mrs. Mubashir Hussain Lahori, to make the book a practical companion for a new bride. It has three parts: kitchen and food tips, beauty tips, and general home remedies.

One note before we begin. These are traditional household tips as the book gives them. For any real health problem, please see a doctor.

# Section 1: Kitchen and food tips — page 578 onwards

## Fixing a curry

Too much salt: put a ball of kneaded dough, or a raw potato, in the curry for a while, then remove it. Or add some gram flour mixed in water, or lemon juice.

Too much chilli: add whole garam masala, such as cinnamon, cardamom, white cumin and cloves, fried in a little ghee. Or add some yoghurt, or a little roasted gram flour.

Too much turmeric: put a piece of white cloth in the curry briefly to absorb it.

Burnt meat curry: move the meat to a new pot, add a little milk, and fry it again.

## Removing smells and bitterness

Fish smell: wash with water containing borax, or rub with lemon juice and salt, or gram flour paste for fifteen minutes, then wash.

Cauliflower smell: add a few drops of lemon juice to the cooking water.

Trotters: add white cumin, cinnamon and cloves while cooking. To clean them, soak in boiled water for three or four minutes, then scrape.

Bitter radish pods, new turnips or karela: peel, salt, rest, then wash.

## Cooking tips

Peeling garlic: soak in hot water for ten minutes. A fine garlic paste: add a pinch of salt while grinding.

Crisp pakoras: add a little garlic paste to the batter.

Softening peas: add mint while boiling.

Tender chickpeas or meat: add two to four potatoes while cooking. If lentils or chickpeas still will not soften, add about half a cup of milk and check after ten or fifteen minutes.

New rice that breaks: wash it two or three times, and soak in warm water before cooking.

Cream from milk: mix about one and a half teaspoons of gelatine into milk, beat well, chill, then skim the cream. Or boil the milk and cool it quickly in an ice water bath, then lift off the cream. A pinch of baking soda keeps cream from spoiling.

Dough: apply a little ghee, keep it covered with a damp cloth, and avoid too much water.

Split milk: strain it to make paneer.

Frozen meat: soak in half a cup of milk for ten minutes before cooking to freshen it, or in salted water to thaw it quickly.

Boiled eggs: add salt to the water, and plunge them in cold water after boiling. To test an egg, a fresh one sinks in water, and a bad one floats. Frying eggs without sticking: sprinkle a little salt in the pan before the oil.

Peeling tomatoes: hold them over a flame on a fork until the skin cracks.

Apples: dip slices in cold salted water so they do not turn brown.

Spluttering ghee: sprinkle a pinch of flour.

## Around the kitchen

Cracked china: boil it in milk on a low flame for about forty five minutes.

Burnt pots: boil water with two spoons of salt, then scrub.

Hot liquids in glass: put a metal spoon in the glass before pouring.

Milk burning in steel pans: rinse the pan with water first.

Rice weevils: mix the rice with whole dried red chillies, or salt.

Clumping salt: add a few cloves and some grains of rice.

Smelly jars: soak with water and a little baking soda, shake, rinse, and keep the lids off. Fridge stains: rub with toothpaste. Freezer odours: keep a little baking soda on the bottom shelf.

Flies in the kitchen: keep mint there.

Pure red chilli: grind it at home, because market chilli is often adulterated.

A burnt hand: immediately apply wet salt or cold water, to prevent blisters. A fish bone in the throat: drink lemon juice, or swallow a lump of dough or banana. Onion smell on the hands: rub with salt and water.

# Section 2: Beauty tips — pages 587 to 590

Acne and blemishes: a paste of lemon peel with glycerine as a mask for fifteen minutes. Or massage the face with the inside of an orange or kinnow peel for about twenty minutes daily. Or wash the face several times a day with warm salt water for ten to fifteen days.

Dark lips: rub with lemon peel.

A glowing face in winter: almond oil and honey on the face and hands at night for half an hour, then wash with warm water.

Freckles: a paste of ground seeds in milk, applied at night and washed off in the morning. The exact recipe is on page 588.

Long, thick hair: a paste of ber leaves for ten minutes before shampooing, and a mild shampoo. For shine, soak mustard seeds overnight and wash the hair with that water. Boiled tea water works as a conditioner. To darken hair, soak dry amla in an iron pot overnight and wash the hair with the water. And for healthy hair, grate bottle gourd, cook it in mustard oil until brown, strain and store it.

Soft skin: massage with lemon juice and olive oil at night. Or a paste of dried orange peel powder and raw milk for an hour before bathing. Or turmeric with cream or milk before bathing.

Facial spots: a teaspoon of lemon juice with a teaspoon of salt, for ten minutes.

Wrinkles: apply milk cream at night, and wash with soap in the morning.

Reducing the belly: lie on your stomach with a pillow under it for fifteen minutes each morning before getting up.

Weight loss: warm water with honey and lemon on an empty stomach, lemon in food, walking and exercise, avoiding sweets, rice and ghee, and eating cucumber, radish, apples and gram flour roti.

# Section 3: General home remedies — pages 591 to 600

## Health

Constipation: a cup of milk with two spoons of honey at night.

Bleeding wounds: press hard and tie a clean cloth.

A child's nosebleed or mouth bleed: put sugar on the spot.

Migraine: drops of almond oil in the nose, or smell a cut onion.

Teething gums: salt and honey. Itching and rashes: a bath with salt and neem leaves, or rub with onion juice.

Strong teeth: roasted alum and salt powder as a tooth powder, two or three times a week. Mouth ulcers: borax with glycerine or honey. Bad breath: chew dry coriander seeds. Chapped lips: fresh milk cream.

Diarrhoea: two or three spoons of sugar in half a glass of water.

Cough: black pepper with salt, about three times a day. Phlegm: eleven raisins with black pepper daily.

Weakness and fatigue: a glass of water with a teaspoon of sugar and a little salt, twice a day.

Foot odour: boil a round brinjal in water and soak the feet daily for a week.

Children's health: almonds, raisins and dried dates ground together, a spoon with milk. Increasing breast milk: soaked white chickpeas eaten with sugar in the morning.

## Around the home

Mosquitoes: burn dried orange peels for smoke. Lizards: hang egg shells near curtains and lights.

Shoe polish: add a few drops of kerosene for shine.

Dark elbows: rub with lemon halves and sugar or turmeric.

Shining gold jewellery: soak in soapy water with a little turmeric for four or five hours, then brush.

A damp, musty home in the rains: burn loban, frankincense.

Darker henna: after it dries, hold the hands over the smoke of cloves on a hot tawa.

Whiter laundry: a little alum or chalk in hot wash water, or a spoon of salt with blue in the rinse water.

Paint smell: cut onions in the room. Chewing gum on clothes: freeze the cloth, then peel it off. Tea stains: salt paste, then detergent after ten minutes. Grease stains: absorbent paper underneath, and spirit on cotton from behind, then wash.

Cleaning glass and mirrors: wash with salt water, or clean with spirit, and dry with newspaper. Cloudy bottles: shake with washing powder and crushed egg shells. Two glasses stuck together: cold water in the top one, hot water around the bottom one.

Keeping vegetables fresh: store them separately in a cool, dry place, and wrap leafy greens in a damp cloth. Preserving tomatoes: boil with salt, blend, and bottle with a layer of oil on top.

Pests: for fleas, garlic and sulphur smoke; for bedbugs, boiling water on the joints and cracks of the bed; for ants, kerosene or turmeric; for termites, neem leaf smoke; and for flies, a bait of black pepper, sugar and milk.
