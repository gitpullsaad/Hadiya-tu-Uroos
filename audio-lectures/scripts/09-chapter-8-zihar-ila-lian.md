# Lecture 9 — Chapter 8: Zihar, Ila and Li'an

Assalamu alaykum. This is Lecture 9, on Chapter 8, pages 451 to 462. This short chapter covers three special situations between husband and wife: zihar, when a husband compares his wife to his mother; declaring one's wife haram; ila, an oath to stay away from one's wife; and li'an, the mutual oath when a husband accuses his wife of adultery without witnesses.

# Section 1: Zihar — pages 453 to 454

Zihar is when a husband says to his wife, "You are to me like my mother's back," or compares her to any woman permanently forbidden to him, such as his sister, daughter, aunt or grandmother. It is a sin, but it does not end the nikah. Expiation, called kaffarah, is required before they resume intimacy. This is from Surah Al-Mujadilah, verses 3 and 4.

So there are four rules. One, the nikah does not break. Two, kaffarah is required before resuming intimacy. Three, intimacy before the kaffarah is a sin. Four, the kaffarah is: free a believing slave; if not possible, fast two consecutive months; and if not possible, feed sixty poor people.

Some notes. The kaffarah is on the man, not the woman. If the consecutive fast is broken without a valid excuse, he must restart. If a husband habitually calls his wife "sister" or "mother" without intending zihar, there is no kaffarah, but he should stop the habit. And if it was done in ignorance, it is forgiven.

## Declaring one's wife haram — pages 454 to 456

What if a husband says, "You are haram for me"? The scholars differ greatly. Ibn al-Qayyim listed thirteen views, Qurtubi eighteen, and Ibn Hajar even more. Some say it is a talaq, some say zihar, some an oath, some that it depends on his intention, and some that it means nothing.

The author's view: unless he intended talaq, it is treated as an oath, requiring the kaffarah of an oath. This was the view of Abu Bakr, Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, Aisha and Ibn Abbas. The evidence: the Prophet, peace be upon him, once declared his slave woman, Mariyah, haram for himself, and Surah At-Tahrim was revealed: "Why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful for you? Allah has ordained for you the dissolution of your oaths." And Ibn Abbas said: "If a man declares his wife haram for himself, it is an oath that he expiates. You have in the Messenger of Allah an excellent example."

# Section 2: Ila — pages 457 to 459

Ila is an oath not to have intercourse with one's wife, usually as a form of discipline. It is allowed for up to four months, by Al-Baqarah verse 226. The Prophet himself swore an ila of one month from his wives. But for more than four months, or to harm her without reason, it is haram, because "there is no harm and no reciprocating harm".

The rules:

One, if he returns to her before four months by breaking his oath, he pays the kaffarah of an oath. The oath kaffarah, from Al-Ma'idah verse 89, is: feed ten poor people, or clothe them, or free a slave. If unable, fast three days.

Two, if he swore for a fixed period of four months or less and completes it, there is no kaffarah.

Three, if four months pass and he neither returns nor divorces, she may go to court, which compels him to either return to her or divorce her. Ibn Umar said that after four months he must either keep her kindly or divorce her, and this was also reported from Uthman, Ali, Abu al-Darda', Aisha and twelve Companions.

Four, the talaq does not happen automatically when four months pass.

Five, if he refuses both despite the court, the court may annul the nikah.

Six, if he divorces her, she observes the normal iddah of talaq.

# Section 3: Li'an — pages 460 to 462

Li'an is when a husband accuses his wife of zina without four witnesses, and she denies it. A court then conducts the li'an, as in Surah An-Nur verses 6 to 9. He swears four times by Allah that he is truthful, and a fifth time invokes Allah's curse on himself if he is lying. She, to avert the punishment, swears four times by Allah that he is lying, and a fifth time invokes Allah's wrath on herself if he is telling the truth.

The rulings, twelve of them:

One, after li'an, the man is spared the punishment for slander, and the woman is spared the punishment for zina.

Two, li'an happens only before a Shar'i court.

Three, the judge first urges them to confess. If one confesses, the punishment applies and there is no li'an.

Four, if the woman confesses, she is stoned.

Five, if the man admits he lied, he receives eighty lashes for slander.

Six, li'an happens only if neither confesses.

Seven, the couple are separated forever and can never remarry.

Eight, a child born after li'an is attributed to the mother.

Nine, the father and that child do not inherit from each other, but the child and the mother do.

Ten, until the li'an is complete, the child belongs to the father, and if the man later admits he lied, the child is attributed to him again.

Eleven, the woman gets no maintenance or housing after li'an.

Twelve, the mahr already given is not returned to him.

## Summary

To sum up Chapter 8: zihar does not end the marriage, but requires a heavy kaffarah before intimacy. Saying "you are haram for me", without intending talaq, is in the author's view an oath, with the oath kaffarah. Ila is allowed for up to four months, after which the husband must return or divorce, with the court able to compel him. And li'an is a solemn exchange of oaths before a court, which spares both from punishment but separates them forever. In the next lecture, we come to special issues for spouses and women, and hijab.
