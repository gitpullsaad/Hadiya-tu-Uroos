# Lecture 8 — Chapter 7: Miscellaneous and Complex Marriage Issues

Assalamu alaykum. This is Lecture 8, on Chapter 7, pages 429 to 452. It deals with some difficult questions: child marriage; the gap between the nikah and the rukhsati; marrying someone much older or younger; and the marriage of people who have committed zina, including marriage during a pregnancy from zina.

As always, I am reporting the book's positions and the author's views as given in the notes.

# Section 1: Child marriage — pages 431 to 435

The book says that in the Prophet's time, guardians married off children, and the rukhsati, when the bride moves to her husband's home, was usually after puberty. Islam kept this practice with a safeguard: the child may annul the marriage after reaching maturity if unhappy.

## The evidence the book gives

Surah At-Talaq verse 4 gives the iddah of "those who have not yet menstruated" as three months. The book takes this, following Ibn Kathir and Maududi's Tafhim ul Qur'an, to imply that the marriage and divorce of a girl before puberty is possible, and Maududi said the Qur'an treats it as permissible. The book also cites Aisha's report in Bukhari and Muslim about her own marriage and its consummation, and Ibn Hajar's report of a consensus that a father may marry off his young virgin daughter.

## The option after puberty

If a child married in childhood is unhappy after puberty, a girl may have the nikah annulled through a court, and a boy may give talaq. Ibn Abbas reported that a virgin girl came saying her father had married her against her will, and the Prophet gave her the choice.

## Pakistani law

The book cites a family law provision from 1991 restricting child marriage, with a minimum of eighteen for boys and sixteen for girls, and fines or jail. The author says this contradicts the Shari'ah and that scholars should review it.

He also quotes Maulana Muhammad Taqi Usmani, a former judge of the Federal Shariat Court, on the wisdom of permitting it. Usmani gives examples: a father sees his child going astray and has a good match available; a terminally ill father wants to secure his daughter's future before he dies; a poor widow with a daughter, at risk of exploitation, finds a decent match; or a peasant fears his landlord's predatory intentions toward his daughter. Usmani argued the law would increase zina and abductions.

# Section 2: Other complex issues — pages 436 to 438

## A gap between nikah and rukhsati

A gap is allowed. After a valid nikah, the couple are husband and wife, so intimacy before the rukhsati is not sinful in principle. But it is socially frowned upon and may expose them to suspicion, and if the girl is very young it should be avoided for medical reasons.

## Marrying someone much older or younger

There is no Shar'i problem with this. Shaykh Ibn Baz advised young women not to refuse an older man who is righteous. Khadijah was forty and the Prophet twenty five when they married. What matters is religion and suitability.

# Section 3: The marriage of those who committed zina — page 439 onwards

## The ruling on zina

Zina is a major sin, with a hadd punishment if proved by four eyewitnesses or by confession before a court: one hundred lashes and a year of exile for the unmarried, and stoning for the married. A pregnant woman is not punished until she gives birth.

## Can the two who sinned marry each other? — pages 440 to 442

If a court has punished them, the punishment is an expiation, and then they may marry. If the matter was never reported, they should conceal their sin, because "whoever conceals a Muslim, Allah will conceal him on the Day of Resurrection", and repent sincerely. Then they may marry each other. "One who repents from sin is like one who has no sin." Ibn Abbas was asked about a man and woman in a haram relationship who both repented and wanted to marry. He said: "Its beginning was fornication, and its end is marriage. There is no harm in it."

## Marriage during a pregnancy from zina — pages 442 to 446

The scholars differ.

The Malikis, the Hanbalis and Abu Yusuf say a woman pregnant from zina cannot be married, by the man responsible or anyone else, until she gives birth, citing "no intercourse with a pregnant woman until she delivers".

The Shafi'is and Hanafis say it is allowed, because that prohibition concerns a woman pregnant from a valid marriage, to protect lineage. And "the child belongs to the bed, and the fornicator gets nothing", meaning the fornicator's seed has no sanctity.

The author's preferred view is with the Shafi'is and Hanafis, but with three conditions. One, only the man by whom she is pregnant may marry her while she is pregnant; no one else until she delivers. Two, both must repent first. Three, if the case reaches a court and is proven, the punishment is applied, after delivery, and marriage is considered afterwards.

His further arguments: there is no evidence prohibiting it, as the prohibition reports concern women in iddah from a valid marriage. A woman pregnant from zina has no iddah, which was the view of Abu Bakr, Umar, al-Thawri, Shafi'i and the Hanafis. The reason for forbidding intercourse with a pregnant woman is "watering another man's crop", and that does not apply when the man marries the woman pregnant by him. And delaying the marriage increases the disgrace for her family, though the child is still not attributed to the fornicator, because "the child belongs to the bed".

## Marrying a known fornicator — pages 447 to 449

Chastity is required in both spouses. A known zani or zaniyah may not be married until they sincerely repent. Marthad al-Ghanawi wanted to marry a prostitute in Makkah, and Surah An-Nur verse 3 was revealed: "don't marry her". Imam Ahmad said a chaste man's nikah with a zaniyah is invalid until she repents, and the same the other way round.

## Summary

To sum up Chapter 7: the book holds that child marriage is permissible in the Shari'ah, with the child's right to annul after puberty, and criticises the Pakistani law restricting it. A gap between nikah and rukhsati is allowed, and a large age difference is no problem in itself. Those who committed zina with each other may marry after repentance, and in the author's view the man responsible may even marry the woman while she is pregnant, with conditions. But a known fornicator may not be married until they repent. In the next lecture, we study zihar, ila and li'an.
