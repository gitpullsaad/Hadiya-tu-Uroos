# Lecture 10 — Chapter 9: Special Issues for Spouses and Women, and Hijab

Assalamu alaykum. This is Lecture 10, on Chapter 9, pages 463 to 500. Much of this chapter is a collection of questions and answers, mostly fatwas of Saudi scholars: Shaykh Ibn Baz, Shaykh Ibn Uthaymin, Shaykh Ibn Jibrin, and the Saudi Permanent Committee. There are three sections: issues for husband and wife, issues for women, and covering and hijab.

# Section 1: Issues for husband and wife — pages 465 to 472

## Does kissing break wudu or the fast?

Kissing or touching one's wife does not break wudu. Aisha reported that the Prophet, peace be upon him, kissed one of his wives and then went to pray without renewing his wudu. Many Companions and Abu Hanifah agreed. The verse in An-Nisa about "touching women" means intercourse, as Ibn Abbas explained.

Kissing and embracing do not break the fast either, unless there is intercourse. The Prophet kissed and embraced while fasting, but he had the most control over his desire. Intercourse during a Ramadan fast requires making up the fast plus a kaffarah: free a slave; if not possible, fast two consecutive months; if not possible, feed sixty poor people. And if a person truly cannot do any of these, it lapses, as with the man who was given dates to give in charity and was told to feed his own family with them.

## Intimacy between spouses

Spouses may see each other's whole body; Ibn Uthaymin cites Al-Mu'minun verses 5 to 7. Ibn Uthaymin also said spouses may enjoy each other in any way except what Allah has forbidden: during menses, anal intercourse, during ihram or i'tikaf, and before the kaffarah of zihar. On a wife's breast milk, a scholar said there is no harm if milk is not swallowed, and even if it is, she does not become forbidden to him, because fosterage counts only in the first two years.

## A husband who does not pray — pages 469 to 470

A woman asked about a husband who abandons prayer and the Ramadan fast. Ibn Uthaymin said that one who abandons prayer altogether is a disbeliever, and the nikah ends. She should not stay with him unless he repents.

## An infertile husband — pages 470 to 471

May a wife seek divorce if her husband is infertile? Yes, Ibn Uthaymin said, because she has a right to children. If he will not divorce her, a judge can annul the marriage.

## Washing each other after death — page 471

It is allowed for a spouse to wash the other's body after death. Asma bint Umays washed Abu Bakr, and Fatimah willed that Ali wash her.

## A separate home — pages 471 to 472

A wife whose brother-in-law lives with them, and who must observe hijab from him: Ibn Jibrin said that if full hijab and avoiding being alone cannot be maintained, separate living is better, and she may request a separate house.

# Section 2: Issues for women — page 473 onwards

## Purity

A nursing baby's vomit: sprinkle water on the spot and pray. A nursing baby boy's urine: sprinkling water is enough. A girl's urine must be washed.

Washing a child's private parts after wudu: the Saudi Committee said touching the private part directly breaks wudu, while Ibn Uthaymin said touching without desire does not, just wash the hands.

Ghusl: there is no difference between men's and women's ghusl. Braids need not be undone for ghusl after intimacy; three handfuls of water on the head are enough.

## Bleeding

Irregular bleeding in a woman of about fifty, when her periods have become irregular, is istihadah, not menses. She prays, fasts, may have intercourse, and makes wudu for each prayer.

Brownish discharge before the usual days is not menses; she should pray. Yellow or brown discharge after a clear sign of purity is not menses either; she prays and fasts.

Pills to delay menses for Hajj or Ramadan are permitted if they cause no harm; consult a doctor. Ibn Uthaymin said it is better avoided for health reasons.

Miscarriage, pages 477 to 479: if the foetus had a human form, with head, hands or feet visible, the bleeding is postnatal bleeding, nifas, and she does not pray or fast until it stops, up to forty days. If there was no human form yet, the bleeding is istihadah, and she prays.

## Nikah and talaq during menses — pages 479 to 481

A nikah during menses is valid. The prohibition during menses is on talaq and intercourse, not on nikah. Talaq during pregnancy is valid, contrary to popular belief. And intercourse with a pregnant wife is allowed.

## Hair, nails and adornment — pages 482 to 486

Women shaving their heads is forbidden, but trimming the length is fine, especially for ease or the husband's liking, without imitating disbelievers or men.

Wigs and hair extensions are haram, even if the husband wants them, because the Prophet cursed those who join hair. The exception is to cover a defect, such as a woman with no hair.

Plucking eyebrows is haram and cursed, and thinning them is not allowed, even at the husband's request. But unusual hair, such as moustache or beard hair on a woman, may be removed. Removing armpit and pubic hair is Sunnah, and women removing leg and arm hair is no problem.

Growing long nails is against the Sunnah, with a forty day limit for trimming. Nail polish must be removed for wudu, because it blocks water.

A paranda, a braid extension made of thread, is permitted.

A nose ring is permitted, like earrings.

High heels are at least disliked, because they deceive about height, risk falling, and are medically harmful.

## Reciting Qur'an during menses — pages 486 to 488

Ibn Baz said a menstruating woman may read du'a books containing verses, and may even recite the Qur'an from memory without touching the mushaf. There is no sound proof forbidding it, and the hadith "a menstruating woman should not read Qur'an" is weak. A person in janabah, however, should not recite, because he can bathe quickly, while menses lasts for days and she may forget what she has memorised.

# Section 3: Covering and hijab — pages 489 to 497

## What is Shar'i hijab

Shar'i hijab means covering the whole body that must be covered. Ibn Baz said the face is the main place of attraction, so covering it is most important. It is strange to claim hijab covers the head, neck, arms and legs, but not the face, when men choose brides by their faces.

The evidence given for covering the face: Al-Ahzab verse 53, asking the Prophet's wives from behind a screen, which is "purer for your hearts"; Al-Ahzab verse 59, drawing down the jilbab; and An-Nur verse 31, not displaying adornment except to the listed relatives.

## Drivers, driving and work — pages 491 to 493

"No man is alone with a woman but Shaytan is the third." A driver alone with a woman is forbidden seclusion. Travelling alone with a non-mahram driver even within the city is not allowed, but with two or more others present it is allowed. Travel requires a mahram, by land, sea or air.

The book reports a Saudi fatwa that women driving is impermissible, because of the evils it leads to, and another that women working alongside men leads to seclusion and fitnah, and is forbidden.

## From whom to observe purdah — pages 493 to 497

This part is from Maulana Abdul Rahman Kilani's book on covering and hijab. He gives five categories.

One, strangers, non-relatives: full hijab, including the face and hands, except in necessity.

Two, mahrams, the men she can never marry: her husband, father, father-in-law, sons, stepsons, brothers, nephews, and paternal and maternal uncles, and their extensions such as grandfathers and grandsons. Foster relatives through breastfeeding count the same. Aisha's foster uncle asked to enter and the Prophet said, "Let him in, he is your uncle." In doubtful cases, observe hijab.

Three, the husband's relatives, such as the brother-in-law and cousins. "Beware of entering upon women." They asked, "What about the in-law?" He said: "The in-law is death." Most negligence happens here.

Four, the wife's relatives, such as the sister-in-law. In Punjab the sister-in-law is joked with as a "half wife", which is gravely wrong. Since a man cannot be married to two sisters at once, she is only temporarily forbidden to him, so hijab is required. And thin clothes are haram.

Five, other relatives, such as cousins. Allah exempted elderly women from strict hijab. The criterion is the age gap and the presence of desire. With children and elderly men strict hijab is not required, though preferable, but if there is desire, hijab is required.

## Summary

To sum up Chapter 9: kissing breaks neither wudu nor the fast; spouses may enjoy each other in every way except what Allah forbade; a wife should not stay with a husband who has entirely abandoned prayer; and she may seek separation from an infertile husband. For women, the chapter gives practical rulings on purity, bleeding, miscarriage, hair, nails and adornment, and allows reciting the Qur'an from memory during menses. And on hijab, the book holds that covering the face is required, and that the husband's male relatives are the most dangerous area of negligence. In the next lecture, we come to polygamy.
